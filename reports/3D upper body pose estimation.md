# Adding Real Depth to SignSure's Arms and Fingers

Yes, SignSure can get 3D shoulders, elbows, wrists and fingers, and it can start this week with no new hardware. MediaPipe already outputs metric "world landmarks" for the body and hands, and RTMW3D is a direct 3D version of the 133-keypoint layout DWPose uses. The catch is accuracy. Monocular depth is learned, not measured, so 3D elbow flexion from a single webcam is realistically good to about **±10–20°**. Multi-view markerless systems reach **2–6° RMSE** against marker-based mocap. SMPL-X/MANO mesh recovery gives anatomically consistent skeletons and the best hand articulation. It does not fix the depth problem, though, and it brings CUDA-only stacks and research-only licences that block a commercial product without a paid licence. The recommended path has three steps. First, add monocular 3D as a feature layer: RTMW3D or MediaPipe world landmarks for the body, with a dedicated 3D hand model attached at the wrists. Second, compare learner and Mixamo reference in a skeleton-agnostic space of joint angles and normalised positions. Third, before any 3D angle is used for grading, run it through the TOST + SPM1d validation SignSure already has, against a two-camera Pose2Sim reference. If 3D elbow angles fail a ±5° equivalence margin, keep 2D angles where the motion plane faces the camera, and offer an optional second-camera mode for high-stakes assessment.

## Monocular 3D keypoints work today, but depth is a learned guess

The cheapest upgrade is already in SignSure's dependencies. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker returns `WorldLandmarks`, "real-world 3D coordinates in meters" with the origin at the hip centre ([MediaPipe pose.md](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md)). Those 3D labels were never measured. Google produced them by fitting the GHUM body model to 2D-annotated images, then having annotators label depth order, which cut depth-ordering errors from 25% to 3% ([BlazePose GHUM Holistic, arXiv 2206.11678](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.11678)). Google reports **36–45 mm body error and 18 mm hand error**, but only on yoga images of a single person 2–4 m away, using 17 COCO keypoints ([arXiv 2206.11678](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.11678)). Waist-up signing with the hips out of frame is out of distribution. A hip-centred origin is poorly defined when the hips cannot be seen. The body and hand world landmarks also live in separate frames, so each hand has to be translated onto the pose wrist before finger positions mean anything in body space.

**RTMW3D** is the closest thing to a "3D DWPose". It is the only maintained, openly licensed single-image model found that outputs all 133 COCO-WholeBody keypoints in 3D. It extends RTMW with a z-branch that predicts depth relative to a root joint ([RTMW paper, arXiv 2407.08634](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634)). It reports **0.056 MPJPE on H3WB**, most likely 56 mm whole-body error given the units of the benchmark's baselines, with 0.678 COCO-WholeBody AP ([MMPose rtmpose3d](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/tree/main/projects/rtmpose3d)). Because it keeps DWPose's keypoint indexing, SignSure's existing indexing and visualisation code carries over. It can be run through MMPose, through an Apache-2.0 PyTorch package ([rbarac/rtmpose3d](https://huggingface.co/rbarac/rtmpose3d/blob/main/README.md)), or as ONNX weights that already run in-browser on WebGPU/WASM and output root-relative metres ([Harvard MMLA RTMW3D tool](https://mmla.gse.harvard.edu/tools/rtmw3d)). Its authors call monocular 3D "ill-posed", and note that scarce 3D whole-body training data limits generalisation ([arXiv 2407.08634](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634)). No per-part breakdown exists, so finger depth quality is unknown and should be assumed weak.

Temporal 2D-to-3D lifters (MotionBERT, MotionAGFormer, PoseFormerV2) reach about **38–47 mm MPJPE** on Human3.6M, and the field has plateaued near 38 mm ([HSTGFormer, arXiv 2608.12187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12187); [MotionAGFormer, arXiv 2310.16288](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16288)). They lift only 17 body joints in H36M format, so DWPose output needs remapping first ([MotionBERT](https://github.com/Walter0807/MotionBERT)), and none of them lift fingers. Whole-body lifting is far worse for hands: the best H3WB lifter has **125 mm pelvis-aligned hand error**, or 44 mm when aligned at the wrist ([H3WB benchmark](https://github.com/wholebody3d/wholebody3d/blob/main/benchmark.md)). The practical lesson is that fingers need a dedicated hand model attached at the wrist, not a whole-body lifter. VideoPose3D should be avoided: it is CC BY-NC and was archived in 2023 ([VideoPose3D](https://github.com/facebookresearch/VideoPose3D)).

For SignSure's elbow work, joint angles are what count. Independent studies point the same way. BlazePose underestimates large elbow flexion angles, and its accuracy depends significantly on the subject's orientation to the camera ([ETH, ICORR 2025](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/62b944dd-2f46-401c-acdc-b9d98ee4d441)). MediaPipe 3D accuracy for the elbow varies strongly with camera azimuth ([Kim et al. 2025, IEEE Access](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fcc7f9ebfa815bc788c0b277ec185521/?utm_source=claude_code)). Across 11 open-source monocular estimators, 3D joint position error was **146–249 mm** against optical mocap ([Rode et al. 2025, Scientific Reports](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a823db339dd65b2ba2abd8a8159e1b2f/?utm_source=claude_code)). One smartphone BlazePose study reportedly found a **+18.9° systematic elbow bias**, though that figure comes from a search summary and was not verified ([MDPI Sensors](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/1/3)). The geometry explains these results. On a 25 cm forearm, a 3–5 cm depth error at the wrist relative to the elbow produces roughly 7–12° of angle error, and signing puts the forearm toward the camera all the time.

## Mesh recovery buys anatomical consistency, not measured depth

Parametric models (SMPL-X for the whole body, MANO for hands) enforce constant bone lengths and plausible joint configurations. Raw 3D keypoints do not. Among SMPL-X regressors, **SMPLest-X** leads its own benchmarks. On UBody, an upper-body video benchmark close to signer framing, its whole-body error is 51 mm and its hand error is 33 mm, against 92 mm for OSX and 147 mm for PyMAF-X ([SMPLest-X, arXiv 2501.09782](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09782v1)). After Procrustes alignment its hand error drops to about **8–11 mm**. Handshape is decent, and most of the error sits in the hand's global position and orientation, which is exactly the wrist rotation that carries meaning in sign language. Meta's **SAM 3D Body** (CVPR 2026) matches the hand specialist WiLoR on FreiHAND (5.5 mm PA-MPJPE). It outputs its own Momentum Human Rig rather than SMPL-X, which sidesteps SMPL-X licensing but means custom retargeting ([SAM 3D Body](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-body)).

For fingers specifically, **WiLoR** beats HaMeR on FreiHAND (5.5 vs 6.0 mm PA-MPJPE) and is about **3× less jittery** with no temporal module (jitter 5.92 vs 20.43 on HO3D) ([WiLoR, arXiv 2409.12259](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12259v1)). Combining a hand model with a body model is the weak point. Pasting independently estimated hands onto a body produces "unnatural wrist orientations and temporal inconsistencies" ([DanceHMR, arXiv 2605.18102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18102)). Image-based whole-body models also jitter on small hand crops.

Three constraints make mesh recovery a research or offline tool for SignSure rather than the main runtime. First, no study was found that validates SMPL-X-derived elbow flexion against a goniometer or mocap, so these angles would need the same validation as monocular keypoints. Second, every method in practice needs an NVIDIA CUDA GPU. SMPLer-X pins PyTorch 1.12 with mmcv 1.7 ([SMPLer-X](https://github.com/caizhongang/SMPLer-X)), and none of these methods document Apple Silicon support. Third, licensing is heavy. SMPL-X prohibits "incorporation in a commercial product" without a licence ([SMPL-X licence](https://smpl-x.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)), which Meshcapade sells from about **€2,000/year** for micro enterprises, with MANO possibly licensed separately ([Meshcapade](https://meshcapade.com/assets/avatar-tech)). WiLoR's weights are CC-BY-NC-ND and it depends on AGPL Ultralytics ([WiLoR](https://github.com/rolpotamias/WiLoR)). GVHMR's weights are non-commercial ([GVHMR](https://pypi.org/project/gvhmr/)). HaMeR's code is MIT, but it still needs MANO ([HaMeR](https://github.com/geopavlakos/hamer)).

## A second calibrated camera is the largest single accuracy gain

Multi-view triangulation changes the error regime, because depth becomes measured rather than inferred. Controlled-task studies put elbow and shoulder flexion/abduction at about **2–6° RMSE** against marker-based mocap. Webcams with differentiable biomechanics reached a median 2–5° RMSE in stroke patients ([Cotton et al., arXiv 2411.14992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14992)). Two cameras gave about 2° elbow error during reaching ([Avni et al. 2023, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.16.532711v1)). Theia3D stayed under 6° for all upper-extremity joints ([Hansen et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2c43500e8df45e6cabffd16853f2ac31/?utm_source=claude_code)). Even so, multi-view systems commonly underestimate peak flexion, and shoulder internal/external rotation remains poor at 15–22° ([Univ. Potsdam](https://publishup.uni-potsdam.de/files/57827/phr809.pdf); [Francia et al. 2026, Applied Sciences](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9fabcdafd4645eb7ac96cfb586d2f6b9/?utm_source=claude_code)).

**Pose2Sim** fits SignSure best. It needs at least two calibrated cameras, uses RTMPose through RTMLib by default, supports the 133-keypoint whole-body model for hands, synchronises cameras by cross-correlating keypoint motion, and runs OpenSim inverse kinematics to output joint angles ([Pose2Sim](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)). Going from 8 to 4 cameras changed angles by under 1° in walking ([Pagnon et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/339cee43060c5ae6a19f3d4368fb9132/?utm_source=claude_code)). Its published marker validation covers the lower limb only, and its whole-body hand model is non-commercial because of a training dataset ([Pose2Sim](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)). OpenCap is a poor fit, since its upper-extremity accuracy is weak and its validation is mostly lower-limb ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2213abd4e01f5014b3deaf0c8158090d/?utm_source=claude_code)).

Depth sensors fall in between. Azure Kinect gave **5–15° upper-joint angle error** against Vicon ([Antico et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8263d82b4a085c54923e18d7d8711357/?utm_source=claude_code)). The device was discontinued in 2023, and its successor is the Orbbec Femto Bolt ([JetsonHacks](https://jetsonhacks.com/2024/07/07/orbbec-femto-bolt-a-microsoft-azure-kinect-replacement/)). iPhone ARKit body tracking averaged **18.8° ± 12.1°** error ([Applied Sciences 2022](https://doaj.org/article/6d61b23a9283450b845f1a3e77c1f072)). One study found monocular MediaPipe matched Kinect v2 against Qualisys, with errors within the goniometer range ([de Sousa Melo et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4708b27dc36b59519c4f1c7da89c8f7e/?utm_source=claude_code)). Depth hardware therefore buys little over a good RGB model, and fingers are too thin to resolve reliably at typical signing distance.

## Sign-language research confirms the split between research and product

Every serious 3D sign-language reconstruction effort since 2023 fits SMPL-X offline, adding sign-specific priors to a regressor's initial estimate. The current best, **DexAvatar** (Dec 2025), reaches **30 mm upper-body and about 13 mm hand vertex error** on the SGNify benchmark. Plain OSX regression without optimisation gets 47 mm and 18 mm ([DexAvatar, arXiv 2512.21054](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21054v1)). DexAvatar runs offline on an RTX 4090, and its authors note that off-the-shelf detectors "often struggle with sign language data". Products and evaluation toolkits, by contrast, overwhelmingly use MediaPipe Holistic, usually in 2D. The WMT 2025 evaluation work acknowledges SMPL-X "would be a visually more expressive choice" but lacks an easy extraction path ([arXiv 2510.07453](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07453)). One study found that **adding MediaPipe depth made sign recognition worse** ([arXiv 2306.17558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17558)). That is a direct warning against assuming 3D beats 2D.

The same literature settles how to compare a learner with a reference. The pose-evaluation toolkit normalises to shoulder midpoint and shoulder width, trims inactive frames, masks low-confidence joints, and uses DTW. Hands-only DTW variants reached 53–55% mAP, against 27% for the older baseline ([arXiv 2510.07453](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07453)). Learner apps such as SIGNU use Procrustes-aligned 3D hand landmarks ([CDSR 2026](https://www.avestia.com/CDSR2026_Proceedings/files/paper/CDSR_141.pdf)). QuickSign splits feedback into handshape, location, orientation and movement ([DEV](https://dev.to/quicksign/building-an-asl-learning-app-that-uses-computer-vision-to-evaluate-your-signs-in-real-time-4j8m)). Retargeting between rigs is fragile. DexAvatar's mocap-to-SMPL-X retarget left finger-length mismatches and could not transfer wrist rotations ([arXiv 2512.21054](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21054v1)). For SignSure, this argues against retargeting the learner onto the Mixamo rig. The better approach is to sample world joint positions and rotations from the FBX and compute the same angles and shoulder-normalised positions on both sides.

## Recommended path: monocular 3D now, gated by SignSure's own equivalence tests

**Step 1: add 3D as a parallel feature stream.** Read MediaPipe `pose_world_landmarks` and hand world landmarks, or run RTMW3D via ONNX to keep DWPose's 133-point indexing. Translate each hand onto its pose wrist. Compute elbow flexion as the angle between the 3D shoulder–elbow and elbow–wrist vectors, and apply One-Euro or Savitzky–Golay smoothing, since single-frame models jitter. Check the z-sign convention empirically: the MediaPipe docs and the TF.js blog appear to disagree on it.

**Step 2: validate before grading.** Record a small set of signers with two calibrated webcams processed through Pose2Sim, plus optical mocap if it is accessible. Run the existing TOST and SPM1d tests on 3D-monocular versus 2D versus multi-view elbow flexion. A ±5° equivalence margin is defensible, because it matches the error range of the goniometer and marker-based benchmarks. The literature suggests monocular 3D will fail that margin for forearm-toward-camera signs and pass for in-plane motion. Report Bland–Altman bias separately from RMSE, because systematic peak-flexion underestimation is common. No published study was found that applies TOST or SPM equivalence to markerless upper-limb angles, so this validation is itself a publishable contribution.

**Step 3: ship a hybrid.** Grade with whichever angle source passed equivalence for each sign class. In practice that means 2D angles when the flexion plane faces the camera, and 3D (or a "use two cameras" prompt) when it does not. Use 2D image-plane hand-to-face distances, normalised by shoulder width, for the location parameter rather than trusting z. Guide camera placement, because elbow accuracy depends strongly on azimuth.

**Step 4: keep mesh recovery as an offline research tier.** Use SMPLest-X with HaMeR (MIT code) on a rented CUDA GPU to audit handshape errors or render a learner "ghost" on an avatar. Do not ship it without a Meshcapade licence. If SignSure ever needs mesh output commercially, evaluate SAM 3D Body's MHR route only after reading the SAM Licence.

## Conclusion

The real bottleneck for SignSure is not getting 3D numbers, which is a one-line API change. It is knowing when those numbers are good enough to grade a learner. The literature has no answer for signing motion. No benchmark measures 3D arm accuracy for signing, and no study validates mesh-derived elbow angles. SignSure's TOST + SPM1d pipeline is therefore unusually valuable. It can turn "3D is probably ±15°" into a per-sign-class decision about where depth is trustworthy, and that decision is more defensible than any model upgrade.

A second implication is that the hand, not the elbow, is where 3D pays off most. Finger articulation from dedicated hand models is already accurate to millimetres after alignment, while arm depth stays ambiguous. Investment should flow toward a strong 3D hand model and skeleton-agnostic comparison against the Mixamo references. Elbow grading should stay anchored to whatever angle source passes SignSure's own equivalence tests.
