# Bound the RMS, Not the Curve, at 15°

Yes, a single take can show statistical equivalence at ±15°, if the 15° bounds the **root-mean-square (RMS) of the suit–video elbow-flexion difference over the sign window**, tested as a one-sided 95% Monte Carlo upper bound below 15°. Mean-only and whole-curve claims both fall short. After today's fixes, the RMS claim's error rates check out against injected truths. A take with no true error passes **100% of the time at 3 px** of keypoint noise. A take whose true RMS sits just outside the margin (−17° injected, true RMS 16.1°) passes **0% at both 3 and 4.5 px**, and the upper bound covers the truth in **every** take (in-house simulation after fixes, 2026-10-29). The follow-up notes raised a false-equivalence concern for negative errors, with an extrapolated 28–36% false-pass rate. That concern is resolved, and the extrapolated figure is superseded. Whether a real take passes is a separate question. That depends on the true disagreement, not on the statistics. At about 3 px the RMS upper bound runs roughly 5° above the truth, so a pass needs a true suit–video RMS of about **9–10° or less**. The closest published analogue, a markerless system against an IMU suit, found a median elbow discrepancy near **12°** ([Francia et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0cd0a4a588bd51eaa1a1ee7e867a5563/?utm_source=claude_code)). The protocol therefore matters more than the test. Seven steps decide whether a real take lands under 9–10° of true disagreement: align the angle definitions, handle the static offset with a pre-registered rule, widen the camera baseline, measure intrinsics, use sub-frame sync, prepare the suit, and measure motion-time keypoint error on labelled frames. Even a clean pass is narrow: it shows agreement with the video reference for this one take, not suit accuracy and not generalisation.

## RMS is the only claim that is both safe and passable at 15°

The pipeline returns three decisions on every call: RMS, mean and whole-curve (the [pipeline](../backend/app/validation/conditional_equivalence.py), lines 395–431). At ±15° the simulations separate them clearly. The **whole-curve claim** is valid but out of reach. Its simultaneous band reaches the margin between 1.0 and 1.5 px of noise. The median band maximum is 14.9° at 1 px and **28.6° at 3 px**, so power falls from 95% at 0.5 px to 55% at 1 px and 0% from 3 px upward ([simulation notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg.md)). Realistic phone footage cannot pass it. The functional-equivalence tests that would make a maximum-deviation claim more efficient, such as Dette & Kokot's sup-norm test, assume many curve realisations, while one take gives one curve ([Dette & Kokot, Biometrika 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/biomet/v108y2021i4p895-913..html)).

The **mean claim** passes too easily and protects too little. It passed 100% of takes with a sine-shaped error whose true RMS was 15° and whose peak was 21°, because positive and negative errors cancelled. Its 90% interval also under-covers. Coverage was 0.80 at 4.5 px and fell to **0.24–0.60** when noise rose only during movement, because the noise estimate taken from the holds cannot see motion-time noise ([follow-up notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg_followup.md)). After the fixes, mean-CI coverage is still only **0.75–0.85 against a nominal 0.90**. In the −17° scenario (true RMS 16.1°) the mean claim still returned EQUIVALENT in **15% of takes at 3 px and 20% at 4.5 px**, while the RMS claim returned none (in-house simulation after fixes, 2026-10-29). Report the mean as a secondary, descriptive bias estimate, never as the headline.

The **RMS claim** is the right headline for three reasons. First, it penalises cancelling errors, which the mean cannot do. Second, it is a single-number agreement statistic in the family that agreement methodology recommends (TDI and coverage probability, [Choudhary & Nagaraja 2005](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~pankaj/choudhary_nagaraja_jspi_2005_preprint.pdf)). Third, its error rates now check out on both sides of zero. RMS does have one honest blind spot. A brief 15° peak in an otherwise clean take (true RMS 5.65°) passed 100% of the time ([simulation notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg.md)). That is correct for an RMS claim, but it means the result says nothing about the worst frame. Report the maximum absolute difference and the percentage of frames outside ±15° alongside it as descriptives.

### The negative-error coverage failure is fixed

The follow-up notes found the RMS upper bound covering the true RMS only 0.72 (3 px) and 0.64 (4.5 px) of the time for a −15° injected error. From that, they extrapolated false-pass rates of 28–36% at the margin ([follow-up notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg_followup.md)). The cause was a systematic positive bias in measured elbow flexion under keypoint noise: +2.0° at 3 px and +3.4° at 4.5 px for that scenario. A positive bias shrinks the magnitude of a negative error, and the RMS was computed on the raw biased difference curve. The pipeline now estimates that bias as the first moment of the Monte Carlo draws and subtracts it from the difference curve before computing the RMS. It still adds the noise variance back, which keeps the bound conservative. Re-run at ±15° with 20 takes per row, every RMS bound covered the truth, and no take outside the margin passed (in-house simulation after fixes, 2026-10-29):

| Scenario (truth) | Noise | P(EQ) RMS | P(EQ) mean | RMS-bound coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| −17° injected (true RMS 16.1°) | 3 px | **0.00** | 0.15 | **1.00** |
| −17° injected (true RMS 16.1°) | 4.5 px | **0.00** | 0.20 | **1.00** |
| +15° injected | 3 px | **0.00** | 0.00 | **1.00** |
| No error | 3 px | **1.00** | 1.00 | — |

All 19 equivalence tests pass after the fix. The false-equivalence concern for the RMS claim is therefore resolved, and the 28–36% figure should not be quoted. One limit remains: 0/20 can only bound the true false-pass rate below roughly 14–17% at 95% confidence. A pre-registered run of 100 or more takes at the margin would tighten that before publication.

## Pass probability depends on noise and on how much true disagreement there is

The table gives the RMS claim's power. Bold rows are post-fix runs. The other rows come from runs before the bias correction. Those earlier figures for zero or positive truth are unaffected in direction, but the exact values may move by a few points and should be confirmed on the final code ([simulation notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg.md); [follow-up notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg_followup.md)). Noise figures are 1080p-equivalent pixels.

| Condition | True RMS | P(pass), RMS claim | Median 95% upper bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5–1.5 px, no error | 0° | 1.00 | 1.9–3.6° |
| **3 px, no error** | 0° | **1.00** | 7.1° |
| 4.5 px, no error | 0° | 1.00 | 10.6° |
| 3 px holds, 4.5 px while moving | 0° | 1.00 | 9.2° |
| 3 px holds, 6 px while moving | 0° | 0.96 | 11.3° |
| 6 px uniform (2 s holds) | 0° | 0.80 | 14.4° |
| 3 px, 1 cm skeleton offset | 3.5° | 0.93 | 8.3° |
| 3 px, +8° constant | 7.9° | 0.93 | 13.5° |
| 3 px, +11° constant | 10.9° | 0.05 | 16.3° |
| **3 or 4.5 px, ±15–17°** | 14.9–16.1° | **0.00** | — |

The upper bound grows about **2.4° per pixel** of noise and sits about 5.4° above the truth at 3 px. That gives two working rules. Keypoint noise must stay at or below about 4.5 px, including during motion, or power drops below 100%. True disagreement must stay below about 9° at 3 px, or below about 5–6° at 4.5 px, for a pass to be likely. The noise rule is within reach. Earlier test-time-augmentation spread on this project's real footage was 1.7–3.7 px, but that is a lower bound on true error, and blur makes motion-time error larger ([PoseBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14367v1)). The disagreement rule is the hard one. Even a ten-camera markerless system shows **7.0–7.4° elbow RMSD with −5 to −6° bias** against markers ([Lahkar et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.939980/full)). IMU suits show elbow biases above 10° ([Choo et al. 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0262730)). If suit and video errors are independent, about 7° each combines to about 10°. A default setup therefore lands near the edge, and a careful setup lands safely inside.

## Seven protocol steps, ranked by how far they move the true RMS

The ranking puts first whatever moves the true disagreement furthest below the roughly 9° pass threshold. Second come the steps that keep the noise model honest enough for the bias correction to work. None of them needs a second performance.

| Rank | Step | Concrete action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Angle definition and skeleton | Compute suit flexion from the **raw suit skeleton's joint positions**, not the Mixamo retarget. Use the same three-point vector definition as the video. Filter both signals identically (6 Hz Butterworth). | Model and segment definitions drive more of the between-system disagreement than sensor error ([Robert-Lachaine et al. 2017](https://pharesst.irsst.qc.ca/etudes-primaires/139)). Raw keypoints alone gave 12–39° errors at some joints ([Uhlrich et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462)). |
| 2 | Offset handling | Before and after the take, record a static pose and a slow full-range elbow flexion clip. Estimate the definition offset from those clips only, and pre-register that rule. Propagate the offset's uncertainty through the Monte Carlo. | Offsets dominate RMSD. After offset removal, all of Theia3D's angles were under 6° ([Wren et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/671c9d767b3c56548b02cb1958ea198f/?utm_source=claude_code)). |
| 3 | Camera geometry | Place the phones about **70–90° apart** (for example ±40–45° about the signer's front), 2–4 m away, at different heights. Keep both arms visible in both views. Use portrait framing with the upper body filling the frame. | Cameras about 28° apart gave RMSE above 10°. Separations of about 69–81° gave the best agreement ([Zhao et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/33bddc7f40ee5b92b2230269cc3da1dc/?utm_source=claude_code)). Joint visibility alone moved error from 14° to 1.6° ([D'Antonio et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5f7f1b3d8dc45222bdcb962e5c6842f7/?utm_source=claude_code)). |
| 4 | Intrinsics and extrinsics | Calibrate each phone's intrinsics offline with OpenCV. Use a checkerboard or ChArUco on a monitor or A4 print, 15–30 tilted poses, in the exact video mode. At the start of the take, film an A4 board where the hands will be. | Two views cannot pin down focal length. Median two-view focal errors are 15–25% ([Kocur et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16304v3)). OpenCap's A4 board gave minimal kinematic loss ([Uhlrich et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462)). |
| 5 | Phone settings | Record at 60 fps, 1080p or higher. Lock exposure with a shutter of 1/500 s or faster under bright light. Lock focus and lens, and turn off stabilisation and auto lens switching. | This cuts blur-driven keypoint error and stops stabilisation from changing the effective intrinsics frame by frame ([Karpenko et al. 2011](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabilization/)). |
| 6 | Sync | Start and end with a clap and a sharp elbow snap. Align coarsely on audio, then refine by cross-correlating **wrist speed**, not elbow angle, at sub-frame resolution. Check drift between start and end, and add the residual offset to the Monte Carlo as a time-shift draw. | Frame-level audio sync leaves up to 1/fps of skew ([arXiv 2210.09821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.09821)). Syncing on the tested angle itself would flatter agreement. |
| 7 | Suit preparation | Check the magnetic field at the performance spot (no more than about ±0.15 G of fluctuation). Keep metal away from the forearms, tighten the straps, and recalibrate on the spot immediately before a short take. Use the static poses before and after the take to estimate drift. | Elbow is a high-bias joint for some IMU suits. Magnetic disturbance and drift raise error ([Rokoko support](https://support.rokoko.com/hc/en-us/articles/44474445861521-Degaussing-your-space-A-guide-to-magnetic-interference); [Smith et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8fef8a8c838c587299bfe3fe6d3ea130/?utm_source=claude_code)). |

Two further steps govern whether the test's error rates are trustworthy, not whether the take passes. Both are mandatory. The first is to **measure motion-time keypoint noise on labelled frames from this take**. Hand-label shoulder, elbow and wrist in both views on 100–200 frames, stratified by wrist speed and occlusion. Double-label about 30 frames to estimate the annotator's own error floor. Feed the error distribution, conditioned on speed, into the Monte Carlo. Triangulating the manual labels also gives a direct label-based elbow curve against the suit. The second step is to **pre-register** the claim (RMS, one-sided 95%, 15°), the sign window, the offset rule, the sync method, the skeleton source, the filter, the frame-exclusion rule, and a tipping-point grid. The grid should rerun the decision with motion noise at 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 times the measured level, and with outlier rates of 0–5%.

Labelled frames matter more after the fix, not less. The bias correction subtracts the bias that the Monte Carlo reproduces from the noise it believes in. When noise rises only during motion, the hold-based estimate stays at the still level. Before the fix, that inflated the observed mean by +3.7° to +5.2° ([follow-up notes](../research_notes/Single%20take%20equivalence%20at%2015%20degrees/simulation_15deg_followup.md)). The correction would then remove too little bias. For a negative true error, that leftover bias again shrinks the observed error, which reopens the anti-conservative direction the fix closed. The post-fix runs used uniform noise and did not test this case. Measuring motion-time noise directly is the safeguard.

## Margin wording that survives review

The literature gives no degree threshold for elbow error that signers perceive. Deaf signers are, however, measurably sensitive to avatar movement quality ([Quandt et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/312f9e9256ac5148bfc7070d66de9c12/?utm_source=claude_code)). The margin must therefore be argued as a measurement-agreement bound, not a perceptual one. Three anchors support 15°. The first is the combined error of two non-gold-standard instruments, about 10° when each contributes about 7°. The second is clinical elbow measurement precision: goniometric smallest detectable differences of about 4–11° ([PubMed 21779866](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21779866)) and a 95% minimal detectable change of 12.5°. The third is the distribution-based elbow-ROM MCID of 14.1°, reported in one surgical cohort alongside an anchor-based MCID of 25° ([Bone & Joint J 2021](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.103B2.BJJ-2020-0259.R3)). Lakens' guidance asks for an a-priori bound in raw units on a named parameter, with its rationale stated ([Lakens et al. 2018](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2788930055)). Draft wording:

> "The equivalence bound was fixed a priori at 15° on the root-mean-square difference in elbow flexion between the suit and the two-phone video reconstruction over the pre-registered sign window. It reflects (i) the expected combined error of two non-gold-standard instruments (published elbow RMSE of roughly 2–7° for IMU suits and about 7° for multi-camera markerless systems, about 10° combined), (ii) clinical elbow-angle measurement precision (goniometric SDD/MDC 4–12.5°), and (iii) the smallest important elbow range-of-motion change reported (14.1°, distribution-based). It is wider than the ±10° used in some IMU agreement studies, because our reference is a two-camera reconstruction with its own error. It is not a perceptual threshold for signers. Because RMS is an average, the maximum per-frame difference and the share of frames outside ±15° are reported separately."

Avoid one tempting phrasing. A 15° elbow error moves the wrist about 6.5–8 cm through a 25–30 cm forearm. That is comparable to the size of sign-location subregions, so 15° must not be presented as a per-frame tolerance for location fidelity.

## What a pass means, and what can still go wrong

A pass supports one statement: *for recorded take X, under the stated and audited video-error model, the RMS elbow-flexion difference between suit and video reference is below 15°, with false-acceptance probability at most 5% under that model, and the decision holds up to k× the measured motion-time noise*. "Video as ground truth" is a framing choice, not a fact. The video is the reference method. Comparing two indirect systems bounds their combined disagreement and does not establish either one's accuracy ([Francia et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0cd0a4a588bd51eaa1a1ee7e867a5563/?utm_source=claude_code)). A pass therefore does not show that the suit is accurate to 15°, that other signs, signers or setups would agree, or anything about population limits of agreement. If the offset was removed, the headline is agreement after a pre-registered definition correction, and the raw RMS must be reported next to it. Never estimate the offset from the sign take itself. Subtracting the take's own mean turns the RMS into a standard deviation and empties the claim.

Several risks remain. All simulations used one motion (`action.fbx`), the right elbow only, and one 35° camera geometry. The post-fix rows have 20 takes each. The noise model is Gaussian AR(1) jitter, while real pose failures are heavy-tailed, confident wrong points ([arXiv 2604.24609](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24609)). The adaptive stillness threshold has not been confirmed by a full Monte Carlo at 6 px with 1 s holds, so use holds of 2 s or longer. The replicate spm1d gate never changed a decision and passed 0% of takes at 3 px or more, so it adds no protection. Report it as a descriptive check only. Sync error and calibration bias toward the focal prior are not yet sampled in the Monte Carlo, and measuring intrinsics removes the larger of the two. Finally, a failed result will not say which system is wrong. A few static poses at goniometer-measured angles, filmed with both systems, would make a failure diagnostic at almost no cost.

## Conclusion

The fixes change where the remaining risk lies. With the RMS bound centred on bias-corrected differences and still inflated for noise, the single-take test is no longer the weak link at ±15°. It is a sound conformity decision whose trustworthiness depends on how good its noise model is. The bias correction makes that dependence sharper. A Monte Carlo that underestimates motion-time noise now under-corrects the bias as well as under-sizing the interval, so labelled motion frames have become a validity requirement, not an optional power booster.

The practical consequence is that a pass is now earned in the recording session, not in the analysis. A ±15° RMS margin leaves about 9° of room for true disagreement at realistic noise. Default suit and two-phone setups use most of that room on definition offsets and narrow baselines alone. A take that follows the protocol, with matched angle definitions, a static-clip offset, a 70–90° baseline, measured intrinsics and wrist-speed sync, should pass if the two systems really agree. If it still fails, that failure is itself informative about the systems.
