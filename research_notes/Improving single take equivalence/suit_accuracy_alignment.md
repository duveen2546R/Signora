# IMU suit accuracy and aligning joint-angle definitions between the suit and video keypoints

Scope: the suit side of the SignSure suit-vs-video agreement study (Rokoko Smartsuit Pro / Pro II exported as Mixamo-skeleton FBX, compared with two-phone RTMW triangulation), and how to align the angle definitions. Evidence is labelled **[Rokoko]** when it is specific to Rokoko hardware and **[general IMU]** or **[markerless]** otherwise. Peer-reviewed Rokoko validation is very thin, so most quantitative evidence is general IMU evidence.

## 1. Published accuracy of Rokoko and comparable IMU suits against optical mocap (elbow, shoulder, wrist)

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed upper-limb validation of the Smartsuit Pro II. The one Rokoko study (first-generation suit, single-plane shoulder and elbow flexion against OptiTrack) is small and its reported metric is ambiguous. In comparable suits, *shape* agreement (correlation, RMSE after offset) is good, typically 2–6°, but **constant biases of 10–21° at the elbow and shoulder are common**. Those biases come mainly from the biomechanical or skeleton model, not the sensors. That matches the 5–24° definition offsets SignSure sees.

### Cited findings
- **[Rokoko]** The only peer-reviewed validation I found of Smartsuit Pro (first generation) joint angles is an IEEE paper, "Investigation of Wearable Motion Capture System Towards Biomechanical Modelling" (2019). It tested shoulder and elbow flexion-extension in vitro (motor-driven) and in vivo against OptiTrack, and concluded from Bland-Altman analysis that the data are comparable to OptiTrack. — [IEEE Xplore 8802208](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8802208/); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335194888_Investigation_of_Wearable_Motion_Capture_System_Towards_Biomechanical_Modelling)
  - **Conflict or ambiguity:** search summaries report the headline metric either as a "Pearson correlation of 0.7° and 0.77°" (correlations have no units) or as an "RMSE below about 0.7°". I could not retrieve the full text to resolve this, so treat the number as unverified. The design covers only single-plane flexion and is not upper-limb fine motion. — same sources
- **[Rokoko]** Mihcin et al. are cited for Smartsuit agreement with optical tracking in gait: maximal bias −1.48 to 2.22° for knee flexion and hip abduction-adduction. This is lower limb only, and I could not confirm the year or reference system. — [search summary pointing to Mihcin et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335194888_Investigation_of_Wearable_Motion_Capture_System_Towards_Biomechanical_Modelling)
- **[Rokoko]** A 2024 locomotion dataset recorded with a Smartsuit Pro 1 only visually checked tracking against static poses and reported no RMSE against a reference. — [Sci Data dataset, PMC11550319](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11550319/)
- **[Rokoko, vendor]** Sensor Fusion 2.0 (hub firmware 2.7.0.3) claims a 24% "increase in quality" in internal testing and less drift over about 10 minutes. Neither "quality" nor the test method is defined, and Rokoko says it "does not completely eliminate magnetic interference". — [Rokoko insights: Sensor Fusion 2.0](https://rokoko.com/insights/sensor-fusion-2-0-more-accuracy-less-drift)
- **[general IMU, Perception Neuron]** Choo et al. (2022, PLoS ONE) compared 10 participants with Vicon over 10 repetitions × 6 tasks (walk, jog, floorball wrist shot). Raw-curve RMSE was small: elbow 1.9–3.9°, shoulder flexion 1.1–2.2°, shoulder abduction 5.4–15.2°. **Bland-Altman mean biases were large and constant: elbow flexion −12.2 to −21.2° in every task**, shoulder abduction −7.5 to +12.2°, shoulder flexion −1.4 to +7.3°. Pearson r was 0.84–0.96 (elbow), 0.59–0.91 (shoulder flexion) and 0.50–0.87 (shoulder abduction). The authors concluded the system may not replace Vicon "if there is a need to replicate raw joint angles". It was calibrated with a 4-pose sequence (steady, A, T, S) and re-zeroed every 5 repetitions. — [Choo et al. 2022, PMC8782534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8782534/)
- **[general IMU, Perception Neuron]** Sers et al. (2019, Measurement) found the system valid to within 5° for most upper-body ROM (neck, thorax, shoulder abduction), with no elbow or wrist measurements. — [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/Validity_of_the_Perception_Neuron_inertial_motion_capture_system_for_upper_body_motion_analysis/9779261)
- **[general IMU, Xsens]** Robert-Lachaîne et al. (2017, Med Biol Eng Comput) put an Optotrak cluster on every Xsens IMU (12 participants). Error attributed to the **biomechanical model was significantly greater (P ≤ .001) than the technological (sensor orientation) error**. Technological error stayed under 5° RMSE in handling tasks, and mean RMSE grew from 1.2° in short functional movements to 2.8° in long complex tasks. — [Unpaywall DOI 10.1007/s11517-016-1537-2](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS11517-016-1537-2); [IRSST record](https://pharesst.irsst.qc.ca/etudes-primaires/139)
- **[general IMU]** Kamstra, Wilmes and van der Helm (2022, Sensors) separated IMU error sources with rigid marker clusters (10 subjects, 9 football movements, lower body). Body-frame definition error was 11.3–18.7° RMSD, soft-tissue artefact 3.8–9.1° and orientation filter 3.0–12.7°. **Segment-frame definition, which is anatomical calibration, was the largest single source.** — [Kamstra et al. 2022, Sensors 22:9765](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/142948797/sensors_22_09765.pdf) (abstract retrieved via Crossref)
- **[general IMU, Xsens]** Shoulder or arm elevation against Vicon: RMSE up to about 12.8° in complex lifting and about 4.7–6.2° in simple flexion trials. A systematic review reports Xsens humeral elevation RMSE at maximum elevation of about 7–43° depending on task. — [Validity of wearable sensors at the shoulder, PMC6514855](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514855/); [Conversion of upper-limb IMU data to joint angles: systematic review, Sensors 23:6535](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/14/6535)
- **[general IMU]** The same review identifies the lack of accepted sensor-to-segment calibration methods as a main barrier, and reports that elbow IMU accuracy improves with functional calibration and kinematic constraints. — [Sensors 23:6535](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/14/6535)
- **[general IMU vs markerless]** In an ETH Zurich 2025 conference paper (elbow flexion; IMUs and BlazePose against optical), Bland-Altman showed **mostly constant deviations for IMUs but non-constant deviations for pose estimation, which underestimated large flexion angles**. Pose-estimation error depended on the participant's orientation to the camera. — [ETH Research Collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/62b944dd-2f46-401c-acdc-b9d98ee4d441)
- **[general IMU vs markerless]** In myoelectric bypass-prosthesis users, IMUs gave more accurate shoulder, neck and torso angles, while markerless (Kinect) was better for elbow angles. — [OSTI 1983135](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1983135)
- **[markerless vs Xsens]** A 2026 Sensors concurrent validation of multi-camera markerless capture against inertial sensors found the best agreement in the upper limb, with shoulder RMSE below 13°. — [Sensors 26:5492](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175492) (full text blocked; summary only)

### Inferences
- The size of SignSure's constant offsets (elbow +11–17°, wrist −11 to −24°) is typical of what IMU suits show against optical references (Perception Neuron elbow bias −12 to −21°; body-frame definition error 11–19° RMSD). The literature attributes such offsets mainly to skeleton or segment-frame definitions, not sensor noise. That supports SignSure's existing treatment of them as definition offsets, not suit error.
- Because IMU errors at the elbow are mostly constant (ETH 2025), while video errors are often angle-dependent, any residual *angle-dependent* suit–video disagreement more likely comes from the video side or from a definition difference that changes with posture (see Q2 and Q6) than from the suit's elbow measurement.
- No published number gives "Rokoko Smartsuit Pro II upper-limb RMSE against optical". Any claim about suit accuracy must lean on general IMU evidence and be worded that way.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed validation of Smartsuit Pro II (or Sensor Fusion 2.0 firmware) against optical capture for elbow, shoulder or wrist angles. The 2019 Rokoko paper's metric could not be verified from the full text.
- No suit validation for sign-language-like fast, small-amplitude hand and arm motion.
- The Xsens single-pose calibration accuracy paper (Robert-Lachaîne et al. 2017, Gait & Posture) was identified, but I did not retrieve its numbers.

## 2. Shoulder: why IMU suits and markerless video disagree on shoulder elevation, how large the disagreement is, and how to mitigate it

### Takeaway
Shoulder elevation is the least consistent angle across all systems. Markerless shoulder flexion against marker-based capture shows about 8–12° RMSD and grows at high elevation. Pose-estimator shoulder joint centres sit about 24–34 mm from marker-defined centres. Neither system models the scapula, so elevation-dependent movement of the "shoulder point" (shrug, scapular upward rotation) enters the two systems differently. A video deficit of 13–24° that appears **only while the arm is raised** is consistent with an angle-dependent (gain or posture-dependent) definition difference, which a constant rest-pose offset cannot remove.

### Cited findings
- **[markerless]** Theia3D against marker-based capture over full arm ranges of motion (J Biomech 2025): overall humerothoracic patterns were similar, but RMSD was about 11.5° for flexion and about 22° for external rotation. Theia3D does not model the scapula. — [J Biomech 2025, S0021929025004178](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021929025004178) (abstract-level summary; full text blocked)
- **[markerless]** Needham et al. (2021, Sci Rep): the **3D shoulder joint centre from OpenPose, AlphaPose and DeepLabCut differed from the marker-based centre by 24–34 mm on average** (walking 24–30, running 27–31, jumping 32–34 mm). The marker-based shoulder centre was the midpoint of medial and lateral markers. No direction was reported for the shoulder; hip and knee offsets were systematic (30–50 mm), which the authors attribute to mislabelled training data. — [Needham et al. 2021, PMC8526586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526586/)
- **[markerless]** In single-camera Azure Kinect against OptiTrack (upper-limb activities of daily living), shoulder elevation bias was +3.5 to +6.5° (Kinect higher), RMSE 8.5–10.9°, and limits of agreement about ±23–30°. The authors link larger errors in complex movements to difficulty estimating joint centres. — [PMC12416023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12416023/)
- **[markerless]** In an OpenPose camera-viewing-angle study, the shoulder was the worst-represented joint (mean percentage deviation 29%) and the most viewpoint-sensitive. — [Sensors 25:799](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/3/799)
- **[general]** Glenohumeral centre-of-rotation estimation error increases significantly beyond about 60° of humeral elevation and is stable during rotations. Predictive (regression) methods are judged unreliable for high-elevation activity. — [search summary of DU faculty paper](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/mat_mech_eng_faculty/35)
- **[general]** Scapulohumeral rhythm: Robert-Lachaîne et al. (2015) show that using only the glenohumeral elevation angle systematically overestimates the glenohumeral contribution, because the scapula moves out of a vertical plane. Shoulder-girdle motion contributes materially to arm elevation. — [Comput Methods Biomech Biomed Eng 2015 (IDEAS record)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/gcmbxx/v18y2015i3p249-258.html)
- **[general IMU]** Soft-tissue artefact on IMU segments is 3.8–9.1° RMSD. For the upper arm, humeral axial rotation is most affected (20–48% of true rotation lost). — [Kamstra et al. 2022](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/142948797/sensors_22_09765.pdf); [search summary on humeral axial rotation STA](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11517-023-02894-z)
- **[general]** A preprint on markerless capture after stroke found reproducible systematic offsets, especially in shoulder abduction. It suggests static-pose alignment, variable-specific calibration or **angle-dependent correction**, validated on independent data. — [Research Square rs-11268533 (preprint)](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11268533/v1)
- **[Perception Neuron vs Vicon]** Shoulder abduction bias was −7.5 to +12.2° and changed sign between tasks; shoulder abduction had the weakest correlations (r 0.50–0.87). — [PMC8782534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8782534/)

### Inferences (mechanisms specific to SignSure's angle definitions; reasoning, not measured)
- **The video shoulder point moves with the shoulder girdle.** The RTMW/COCO shoulder keypoint is a surface-landmark-like point near the acromion, not the glenohumeral centre. When the arm is raised, the clavicle and scapula elevate and upwardly rotate, so the keypoint rises. With a humerus of about 30 cm, a 3 cm rise of the proximal end tilts the shoulder→elbow vector by about 5–6° toward horizontal at 90° abduction, which *reduces* video elevation. The suit's upper-arm bone direction comes from the upper-arm IMU orientation, so a shrug barely changes the suit's humerus direction. This alone could explain a meaningful part of the 13–24° deficit, and it is zero at rest, which is why the rest-hold offset does not catch it.
- **The trunk axis tilts toward the raised arm in video.** SignSure's trunk axis uses the mid-shoulder keypoint. A raised arm lifts the ipsilateral shoulder keypoint, shifting the mid-shoulder point and tilting the video trunk axis toward the raised arm. That reduces upper-arm–trunk elevation further, by a few degrees. The suit's trunk axis comes from spine bones driven by the back IMUs, which do not see a one-sided shrug.
- **Mixamo "Shoulder" (clavicle) bone.** The suit FBX has a clavicle bone driven by a shoulder IMU. If the elevation angle uses the suit's *arm* bone origin (which moves with the clavicle bone) but the *arm* bone direction (from the upper-arm IMU), suit elevation stays humerothoracic. Video, in contrast, mixes in girdle elevation through the moving keypoint. **Mitigation:** compute the trunk axis in both systems from points that do not move with the shoulder girdle (pelvis midpoint → neck/thorax point, or a thorax frame built from the hips and a fixed-at-rest shoulder-width vector). Or make the suit's upper-arm proximal point emulate the video by using the FBX clavicle-bone endpoint closest to the acromion, so both systems include the same girdle contribution.
- **Calibration-pose definition of "zero".** The Rokoko straight pose (I-pose, arms touching the thighs; see Q4) defines the arm bones as hanging vertically. A real humerus in that pose is slightly abducted because the hips hold the hands out, so the suit's arm bone rest direction is a rig convention. This produces a constant offset (already handled by the rest hold) but can also interact with the elevation axis as a small gain error at large elevations.
- Because the disagreement appears in arm-raised holds and not at rest, a **two-point (rest + raised) or range-of-motion calibration** is needed to tell offset from gain (see Q5–Q6).

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly compares Rokoko (or any IMU suit) shoulder elevation with RTMW/COCO keypoint triangulation, and no published direction or size for the shoulder-keypoint displacement during arm elevation (Needham reports magnitude only, not direction).
- No published Xsens or Rokoko validation stratified by elevation angle (for example error at 0–30° against 60–120°).
- The Theia3D shoulder paper's bias direction at high elevation could not be read (paywalled).

## 3. Wrist and hand: IMU glove against no glove; what the suit hand bones contain; accuracy of wrist angles

### Takeaway
A standalone Smartsuit Pro II has **one IMU on the back of each hand**. Without Smartgloves the fingers are not tracked, so finger and knuckle bones are rigid with the hand bone in its rest geometry. The suit's "wrist deviation" is then the forearm-IMU vs hand-IMU orientation, read through a rig-defined hand axis. The only Rokoko-specific wrist study (Smartgloves I, IEEE BSN 2026) found the default wrist flexion axis **35–39° away from the functionally calibrated axis** (between-person spread about 8°), with near-total axis crosstalk (|r| ≈ 0.99) until functional calibration was applied. That is the same mechanism and order of magnitude as SignSure's −11 to −24° wrist offsets and noisy wrists.

### Cited findings
- **[Rokoko]** Smartsuit Pro II standalone streams 19 sensors: head, hips, upper and lower legs, feet, lower back, shoulders, upper arms, lower arms and **one hand sensor per side**. A glove-ready suit streams 17, with Smartgloves replacing the two hand sensors. The default stream rate is 100 Hz. The page describes no finger tracking without gloves. — [Rokoko SDK: Smartsuit Pro II](https://sdk.rokoko.com/reference/products/smartsuit-pro-ii/)
- **[Rokoko]** "Functional-Axis Calibration of Rokoko Smartgloves for Wrist Coupling Measurement: A Benchmark Against Optical Reference System" (IEEE BSN 2026, Amrita/AMMACHI Labs, abstract only):
  - Consumer gloves expose no forearm landmarks, so the wrist axes come from rig rest-pose geometry, which need not match the anatomical axis.
  - The default flexion-extension axis sat 35–39° from the functional axis, with about 8° between-participant spread.
  - Uncorrected flexion and radial-ulnar deviation were coupled at |r| ≈ 0.99, and functional calibration removed this.
  - Residual off-axis leakage remained in every task and was larger for radial-ulnar-dominant movements.
  - Peak range of motion matched optical norms.
  - Conclusion: functional axis calibration is a prerequisite for wrist biomechanics with entry-level IMU gloves.
  - The study tested first-generation Smartgloves. — [Amrita University news item](https://www.amrita.edu/?p=697902)
- **[Rokoko]** Smartgloves have no calibration command and rely on automatic initialization. — [Rokoko SDK calibration guide](https://sdk.rokoko.com/guides/calibration/)
- **[Rokoko, vendor]** Smartgloves II (launched February 2026) claim "3×" better wrist tracking (1.5 mm → 0.47 mm at fingertip distance) and a magnetometer to remove heading drift. The test method is not stated. — [Rokoko Smartgloves II product page](https://rokoko.com/es/products/smartgloves-ii); [Rokoko insights: Smartgloves II](https://www.rokoko.com/insights/smartgloves-ii-precision-finger-hand-capture-redefined)
- **[Rokoko, independent comment]** A 2025 Sensors review comment notes commercial gloves such as Rokoko's have limited dynamic validation and inconsistent specification reporting. — [Sensors 25:367 review report](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/2/367/review_report)
- **[general IMU]** The Perception Neuron full-body validation reported no wrist joint angles, and I found no peer-reviewed wrist-angle validation of an IMU *suit* hand sensor against optical capture. — [PMC8782534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8782534/)

### Inferences
- **First, check whether gloves were used, from the data.** In the FBX, compute the across-frame variance of the local rotations of finger bones (`mixamorig:LeftHandIndex1`, etc.). If it is about zero, there were no gloves: the "knuckle centre" is a fixed point on the hand bone and the wrist angle is purely forearm-IMU vs hand-IMU, set by the rig's rest-pose hand axis.
- **Why the wrist offset is so large.** With no gloves, the suit hand vector (wrist → knuckle centroid) is fixed by the actor-skeleton rest geometry and the straight-pose calibration (hands against the thighs, which forces some ulnar or radial deviation and flexion). Video measures the actual knuckle positions. A per-person constant of −11 to −24° is the expected result, comparable to the 35–39° axis offset in the glove study.
- **Why the wrist is noisy.** On the video side, hand keypoints are small and often occluded. On the suit side, a single hand IMU is subject to magnetic disturbance near the body or metal, and the BSN study shows crosstalk between flexion and deviation when axes are rig-defined. An unsigned forearm-vs-hand angle mixes flexion and deviation, so the crosstalk is not removed by SignSure's unsigned definition; it is simply folded in.
- **Mitigation.** Treat the wrist as a lower-tier angle (SignSure's fixed-sequence order already puts it last). Add a short functional wrist trial (slow flexion-extension, then radial-ulnar deviation, forearm held still) to the recording protocol, used to fit each system's functional wrist axis or at least a per-angle offset and gain. If gloves were not worn, report wrist results as "hand-segment orientation" rather than "wrist deviation".

### Gaps
- Whether the SignSure takes (validationDataset/After, Action) used Smartgloves is unknown. The finger-bone variance check above would settle it.
- No full text was available for the BSN 2026 glove paper (RMSE against optical not retrieved). No data on Smartgloves II or on the standalone-suit hand sensor's wrist accuracy.

## 4. Suit calibration procedures (pose, magnetic environment, drift, recalibration) and their effect on angle error

### Takeaway
Rokoko calibrates with a single static **straight pose** (I-pose: upright, arms at the sides touching the thighs, about 3 s to get in position and 2 s still). The calibration sets sensor orientation against gravity and the magnetic field. Rokoko gives a magnetic stability guideline (fluctuation about ±0.15 G) but no official recalibration interval. In the literature, static single-pose calibration defines each segment's zero orientation, and errors in that definition dominate IMU joint-angle error (11–19° RMSD in Kamstra 2022). So calibration-pose quality maps directly onto the constant offsets SignSure sees.

### Cited findings
- **[Rokoko]** Calibration sets each sensor's orientation relative to gravity and the local magnetic field. Sensors must be at a standstill, away from large metal objects and magnetic interference. It is triggered with the `CalibrateStaticPose` command. — [Rokoko SDK: Smartsuit Pro II](https://sdk.rokoko.com/reference/products/smartsuit-pro-ii/)
- **[Rokoko]** Studio maps sensors onto a skeleton and calibrates the user's body to the sensors internally. The SDK calibrates sensors only. — [Rokoko SDK calibration guide](https://sdk.rokoko.com/guides/calibration/)
- **[Rokoko]** The straight pose (I-pose) is a relaxed upright stance with arms at the sides touching the thighs, held still during a countdown. The setup guide calls calibration "arguably the most important step" and says not to rush it. That guide is for Studio Legacy, which is no longer supported. — [Rokoko support: Getting Started (Legacy)](https://support.rokoko.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410415520529-Getting-Started-Rokoko-Legacy); [Reallusion manual: Rokoko device settings](https://manual.reallusion.com/motion-live-2d/cta/body-mocap/rokoko-for-cta.htm)
- **[Rokoko, anecdotal]** By default the calibration gives 3 s to get in position and 2 s of stillness. One reviewer found recalibration during a session was not needed but did it occasionally "just to be safe". — [No Film School review](https://nofilmschool.com/rokoko-review-high-quality-mocap-fraction-cost); [HKU "How to Rokoko" guide](https://bookstack.hku.nl/books/motion-capture-motion-tracking/page/how-to-rokoko)
- **[Rokoko]** Magnetic interference shows as yellow sensors in Studio. The Smartsuit Pro needs a stable field within about ±0.15 G fluctuation. Symptoms include inaccurate movements and drift, for example a foot pointing sideways. — [Rokoko support: Degaussing your space](https://support.rokoko.com/hc/en-us/articles/44474445861521-Degaussing-your-space-A-guide-to-magnetic-interference)
- **[Rokoko]** Sensor Fusion 2.0 firmware reduces but does not eliminate magnetic drift. Pre-update firmware "drifts significantly" over about 10 minutes. — [Rokoko insights](https://rokoko.com/insights/sensor-fusion-2-0-more-accuracy-less-drift)
- **[general IMU]** Perception Neuron validation re-zeroed (recalibrated) the system after every five repetitions of each task. — [Choo et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8782534/)
- **[general IMU]** Static calibration quickly becomes invalid for IMUs loosely mounted on the body. — [Joint-constraint dynamic calibration, PMC8588210](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8588210/)
- **[general IMU]** Body-frame definition error (11.3–18.7° RMSD) exceeded soft-tissue artefact (3.8–9.1°) and orientation-filter error (3.0–12.7°). — [Kamstra et al. 2022](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/142948797/sensors_22_09765.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Protocol for SignSure takes:**
  - Calibrate (straight pose) immediately before each validation take, not once per session.
  - Hold the straight pose consistently: palms against the thighs, head neutral, feet parallel.
  - Do the calibration in the same spot as the performance, away from the laptop, desk legs and rebar-heavy floors. Confirm no yellow sensors in Studio.
  - Keep takes short (well under the about 10 minutes over which Rokoko shows drift).
  - Record a still hold at the start *and* end. Comparing the suit–video offset in the two holds gives a per-take drift check: if the offset changes by more than a few degrees between holds, the take's offset model is suspect.
- Suit drift during a take shows up as a *time-varying* offset. SignSure's pre-sign-hold offset assumes a constant offset, so the end-of-take hold is the natural non-circular test of that assumption.
- The Rokoko glitch frames at the start or end of exports (snap to a reference pose) are plausibly the calibration or reference pose leaking into the clip. Keeping "Include reference pose" off (already the project convention) and trimming them (`trim_glitches`) is consistent with that. I found no Rokoko documentation on this behaviour.

### Gaps
- No official Rokoko guidance on recalibration frequency, calibration-pose tolerances, or quantified angle error from a poor calibration pose.
- No published measurement of Smartsuit Pro II drift in degrees per minute.

## 5. Methods for aligning skeleton definitions between systems (functional joint centres, anatomical poses, segment alignment, static pose plus range-of-motion trials)

### Takeaway
The literature agrees that most inter-system joint-angle disagreement is a **segment-frame definition** problem. The standard fixes are (a) a static reference pose to set constant offsets, (b) **functional calibration movements** (slow single-axis range-of-motion trials) to define functional axes, and (c) identical joint-centre definitions in both systems. The Rokoko glove study shows functional-axis calibration removing a 35–39° axis error. I found no published method that maps an IMU-suit skeleton onto a 2D/3D keypoint skeleton specifically. The defensible approach is to compute both from the same point set and calibrate residual offsets and gains on dedicated calibration trials, not the sign.

### Cited findings
- **[general]** ISB recommendations (Wu et al. 2005) define the shoulder and arm joint coordinate systems: thorax frame from C7, T8, IJ and PX; humerus from the glenohumeral rotation centre (estimated by regression or functional methods) to the epicondyle midpoint; YXY decomposition for humerothoracic elevation. *This paper predates 2018 and was not re-fetched in this session; it is cited as the standard reference.* — [Wu et al. 2005, J Biomech, doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.05.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.05.042)
- **[general IMU]** Functional calibration and kinematic constraints improve elbow IMU accuracy. Sensor-to-segment calibration is the main unsolved barrier. — [Sensors 23:6535 systematic review](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/14/6535)
- **[general IMU]** A practical sensor-to-segment calibration method for upper-limb inertial capture in clinical settings has been proposed (2024); details were not retrieved. — [ResearchGate 385477807](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385477807_A_Practical_Sensor-to-Segment_Calibration_Method_for_Upper_Limb_Inertial_Motion_Capture_in_a_Clinical_Setting)
- **[Rokoko]** Functional-axis calibration of Smartgloves (a slow single-axis wrist motion defines the axis) removed a 35–39° default-axis error and |r| ≈ 0.99 crosstalk. — [Amrita news item, IEEE BSN 2026](https://www.amrita.edu/?p=697902)
- **[general]** Wrist-placed IMUs gave more accurate elbow flexion than forearm-placed ones, and elbow flexion was overestimated by about 28° under one calibration pose. Calibration-pose choice alone can create offsets of this size. — [search summary of Northumbria/Derby IMU placement study](https://journals.northumbria.ac.uk/index.php/gjsscmr/article/view/1548)
- **[markerless]** Markerless pose estimators' shoulder joint centres differ from marker-based centres by 24–34 mm. Hip and knee offsets of 30–50 mm are systematic and attributed to training-label definitions. Keypoints are *label-defined* points, not anatomical joint centres. — [Needham et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526586/)
- **[markerless]** The stroke preprint recommends static-pose alignment, variable-specific calibration or angle-dependent correction, **validated on independent data**. — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11268533/v1)

### Inferences (concrete alignment steps for SignSure)
1. **Use the same point semantics on both sides.** This is already done for the hand (knuckle centre). Extend it:
   - **Shoulder point:** use the FBX point that best corresponds to the COCO shoulder label (the acromion region, at the distal end of the clavicle bone), not the Mixamo `Arm` bone head (glenohumeral centre). Or move the video shoulder point inferomedially by a fixed per-person vector measured in the still hold. Either way the two systems see the same shrug contribution.
   - **Trunk axis:** build it from points that do not move with arm elevation (hip midpoint → a fixed thorax point), or from the rest-hold shoulder midpoint carried rigidly with the thorax.
2. **Add a pre-registered calibration block to every take, recorded before the sign:**
   - the existing still hold (offset);
   - a held **arm-raised pose** (each arm at about 90° abduction and about 90° flexion for 1–2 s);
   - a **slow elbow bend**;
   - a **slow wrist flexion and deviation** sweep.
   These give at least two levels per angle, enough to fit offset + gain (Q6). They lie outside the sign window, so estimating from them is not circular.
3. **Functional joint centres (optional, more work).** Estimate the glenohumeral centre in both systems from the arm circumduction in the calibration block (a sphere fit of the elbow trajectory relative to the thorax). Then define the upper-arm vector from that centre in both. This removes the keypoint-vs-bone-head discrepancy at its source. *The SCoRE/sphere-fit methods are background knowledge and were not verified in this session.*
4. **Pre-register the alignment model**: offset only, or offset + gain per angle; which calibration poses; and the acceptance rule. Do this in `equivalence_margins.json` before the next take, as the project does for the offset rule.

## 6. Are the offsets constants or gain (angle-dependent) errors, and how can they be estimated without circularity?

### Takeaway
Evidence suggests **IMU-side elbow errors are mostly constant**, while **markerless errors are often angle-dependent** (they underestimate large flexion, and shoulder-centre error grows above about 60° elevation). SignSure's shoulder-elevation deficit, which appears only in arm-raised holds, therefore looks like a gain or posture-dependent term. A single-hold offset cannot remove it. The standard statistical tool is a Model II (Deming or orthogonal) regression of one system on the other, fitted to **independent calibration data**, giving an intercept (constant bias) and slope (proportional bias). Ordinary least squares is biased (attenuated) when both systems have error.

### Cited findings
- **[general IMU vs markerless]** IMU elbow flexion deviations were mostly constant, while pose-estimation deviations were non-constant, underestimating large flexion. — [ETH Research Collection 2025](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/62b944dd-2f46-401c-acdc-b9d98ee4d441)
- **[general]** Glenohumeral centre error rises significantly above about 60° humeral elevation. — [DU faculty paper (summary)](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/mat_mech_eng_faculty/35)
- **[Xsens]** In a basketball layup study, Xsens may underestimate angles as movement magnitude increases, which is an angle- or amplitude-dependent error. — [Applied Sciences 15:3847](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15073847)
- **[methods]** When proportional bias is present, Bland-Altman limits can be replaced with regression-based limits. Model II (Deming) regression estimates both fixed (intercept) and proportional (slope) bias. — [Türkiye Klinikleri J Biostat methods paper](https://turkiyeklinikleri.com/article/en-iki-olcum-tekniginin-uyumunu-incelemede-kullanilan-grafiksel-ve-regresyon-yontemleri-uygulamali-ve-senaryolara-dayali-bir-calisma-64364.html)
- **[methods, non-peer-reviewed]** The slope is read as the proportional-bias term (CI checked against 1) and the intercept as the constant-bias term (CI against 0). OLS gives attenuated slopes when X has error, and weighted Deming is needed if error variance is not constant. — [MetricGate Deming docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/deming-regression/)
- **[general IMU]** IMU-versus-goniometer comparisons found proportional bias (OLP regression) for pelvis and sternum angles. — [search summary, PMC11511509 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11511509/table/sensors-24-06580-t004)
- **[markerless]** Corrections for systematic markerless bias should be validated on independent data. — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11268533/v1)

### Inferences (how SignSure could do this without circularity)
- **Diagnosing offset vs gain.** Compare the suit−video difference in the pre-sign rest hold, the post-sign hold, and any arm-raised hold.
  - The same difference in all holds means a constant offset.
  - A difference that grows with angle level means gain.
  - A difference between the pre- and post-sign rest holds means drift.
  The After take already shows a 13–24° larger deficit in arm-raised holds than at rest, which is direct evidence of a non-constant term for shoulder elevation.
- **Estimating without circularity:**
  1. Estimate offset and gain only from calibration-block frames recorded **before the sign**, never from the sign window.
  2. Use orthogonal or Deming regression with the error-variance ratio taken from the noise model already estimated (`conditional_equivalence.py`: still-frame noise per system). This is the same externally estimated error model the RMS test uses.
  3. Re-estimate offset and gain inside every Monte Carlo draw, as is already done for the offset, so their uncertainty propagates into the RMS upper bound.
  4. Pre-register the functional form (linear, two parameters per angle) and the poses before analysing a take. Applying it to After and Action is exploratory, as the existing CLAUDE.md conventions already require for post-hoc choices.
- **Risk of over-correcting.** A gain fitted on the calibration block absorbs genuine suit error at high angles as well as definition differences. Two safeguards keep the claim honest: report raw, offset-only and offset+gain RMS side by side, and cap the allowed gain range in pre-registration (for example 0.8–1.25). Wording should stay "agreement after a pre-registered definition alignment", consistent with the project's "not suit accuracy" caveat.
- **Fix definitions first.** A definition fix (same shoulder and trunk points in both systems, Q5) is preferable to a statistical gain, because it removes the posture-dependent term at its source. Use the gain model only for what remains.

- No sources were found on how many calibration poses or levels are needed to estimate a per-angle gain to a given precision. A simulation with the existing `single_take_simulation.py` could answer this.
