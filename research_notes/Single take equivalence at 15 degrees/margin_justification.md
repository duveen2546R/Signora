# Justifying a ±15° Equivalence Margin for Elbow Flexion (Mocap Suit FBX vs Video), and Which Statistic It Should Bound

Research notes. Search setup: the Consensus MCP was unstable (the first calls failed with ECONNRESET, then it reconnected and returned 3 results per query), so most evidence comes from web search. Several key papers were seen only as abstracts or snippets, and this is flagged where it matters. Values the caller gave from earlier research (Choo 2022, Li 2024, Lahkar 2022, Ceccarelli 2026) are labelled "(caller-provided)". I did not re-verify them here.

## Q1. How much arm/elbow angle variation do sign languages tolerate? (phonology, recognition, avatar perception, natural variability)

### Takeaway
No study I found gives a degree-valued threshold for elbow-angle error that signers notice or that harms comprehension. That is the biggest gap. The indirect evidence points in two directions. Sign phonology encodes location and movement in coarse categories, and the same signer produces large, context-driven phonetic variation in arm position, which suggests some tolerance. But Deaf and early-acquiring signers are measurably sensitive to avatar movement quality, so a margin cannot be called "imperceptible" without its own perceptual test.

### Cited Findings
- **Large natural variation in arm position within a signer (sign lowering).** Faster signing causes undershoot of location and handshape (Mauk 2003). Tyrone & Mauk (2010) studied lowering of forehead-located signs such as WONDER — [Mauk & Tyrone review, PMC4489421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4489421)
- In motion-capture recordings of native signers, all signers produced lowered forms under the manipulated phonetic conditions, and some effects of signing rate were unexpected — [Tyrone & Mauk 2012, Language and Speech, doi:10.1515/lp-2012-0019](https://doi.org/10.1515%2Flp-2012-0019)
- In a corpus of about 3,000 ASL signs, location lowering was mostly gradient and driven by lexical frequency, with a categorical process in a small set of signs. Signers avoided the eye area — [Russell, Wilkinson & Janzen 2011, summary at UNM](https://www.unm.edu/~ewilkins/article/research/undershoot-aka-sign-lowering-asl/)
- Sign lowering depends heavily on context (sentence position, coarticulation, prosody). One STEM-ASL dialogue dataset found no significant vertical hand-position difference from vocabulary baselines — [arXiv 2510.23842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23842)
- **Kinematics vary systematically with discourse type.** In Finnish Sign Language narratives, sequences with more constructed action had smaller movement prominence (peak hand–torso distance), lower complexity, and lower velocity and acceleration — [Vandenitte et al. 2026, Language and Speech](https://consensus.app/papers/details/cdfb702d21a3520caec554c6972deea9/?utm_source=claude_code)
- The kinematics of lexical signs differ significantly from constructed action and classifier forms (Israeli SL, Kinect mocap) — [Stamp et al. 2023, Language and Speech](https://consensus.app/papers/details/acb031eb96cc5b0da2f4180353de0f44/?utm_source=claude_code)
- **Repeatability between sessions.** In 3 signers (Deaf, proficient, student), most kinematic features (duration, path length, signing-space volume, mean and peak velocity) had good-to-excellent ICCs across repeated sessions. The authors say quantitative methods for sign variability are "yet to be clearly defined". This is a bioRxiv preprint with n=3, and the abstract gives no joint-angle SDs in degrees — [Harbour et al. 2026, bioRxiv](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7c7fb37e60df5756b942a84dd42cc210/?utm_source=claude_code)
- **Signers are sensitive to avatar movement quality.** 191 ASL users rated a mocap-driven avatar significantly higher than a computer-synthesised avatar on attitudes, impressions, comprehension and naturalness. Signers who acquired ASL earlier were the most sensitive to movement-quality problems — [Quandt et al. 2022, Frontiers in Psychology](https://consensus.app/papers/details/312f9e9256ac5148bfc7070d66de9c12/?utm_source=claude_code)
- **Positional error vs perception.** 25 users compared a mocap-based avatar with a version that differed by millimetre-range total positional error (generated from ML synthetic data). The authors found "statistically significant inter-dependencies" between avatar movement and perception, but could not identify which concrete features drive perception. Abstract only; no degree threshold reported — [Quantifying Sign Avatar Perception: How Imperfect is Insufficient?, CHI EA 2020, doi:10.1145/3334480.3375222](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3334480.3375222)
- **Delta testing.** Kipp, Heloir & Nguyen (2011) proposed measuring avatar comprehensibility relative to a human signer. It is a comprehension-based method and gives no joint-angle tolerance — [DFKI publication page](https://www.dfki.de/web/forschung/projekte-publikationen/publikation/5469); [SLTAT 2011 feasibility study](https://vhg.cmp.uea.ac.uk/demo/SLTAT2011Dundee/5.html)
- Avatar reconstructions from monocular video with linguistic priors (SGNify) were rated as comprehensible and natural as the source videos in a perceptual study. This shows that video-derived 3D poses, which have their own error, can still support comprehension — [Forte et al., CVPR 2023](https://is.mpg.de/publications/forte23-cvpr-sgnify)
- A 2026 expert (interpreter) assessment of a bilingual avatar gave a mean of 5.5/7 and named naturalness of movement and consistency of transitions as the main weaknesses — [Amangeldy et al. 2026, Sensors](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f580e0f073585bceb299f0467399f54d/?utm_source=claude_code)

### Inferences
- Location and movement are contrastive at the level of body regions and paths, not degrees. Within-signer lowering shows that arm configuration shifts substantially without changing sign identity. This is a qualitative argument that a few degrees to about 15° of elbow error is unlikely to change which sign is perceived. It is an argument, not a measured threshold.
- The perception literature (Quandt 2022; CHI EA 2020) warns that naturalness can suffer at errors well below what changes sign identity. ±15° should be justified as a measurement-agreement margin, not as a perceptual or "imperceptible to signers" margin, unless the project runs its own perception check, for example delta testing with the suit vs video avatar.
- Elbow flexion feeds into hand position through the forearm length (about 25–30 cm). A 15° elbow error moves the wrist by roughly 2·L·sin(7.5°) ≈ 6.5–8 cm, which is comparable to the size of sign-location subregions such as chin vs mouth. This is my own geometric estimate and not from a cited source. It argues against using ±15° as a frame-by-frame worst-case tolerance when the claim concerns location fidelity.

### Gaps
- No study found that reports a just-noticeable difference, or a comprehension-drop threshold, in degrees of elbow (or any arm-joint) error for signing avatars.
- No sign-language mocap dataset found that reports within- or between-signer SDs of elbow flexion angle across repetitions in degrees. Harbour et al. 2026 (preprint) is the closest but reports ICC/CV of other features. The Alexanderson & Beskow and Huenerfauth (CUNY/RIT) evaluation literature was not reached in this search budget. It may contain motion-quality vs comprehension data and is worth a targeted look.
- No study found on how sensitive sign-recognition models are to injected joint-angle noise in degrees.

## Q2. What margins/thresholds are used in upper-limb kinematic validation?

### Takeaway
Clinical measurement error for the elbow alone is roughly 4–12.5° (MDC/SDD). The only elbow-ROM MCID found is 14.1° (distribution-based) or 25° (anchor-based) in one surgical cohort. Ergonomic tools use angle bands of 20–60° width. Prior device-agreement work uses ±10°. On this evidence, ±15° sits at the lenient end of the range for agreement between two instruments, and the strongest justification is the combined error of two imperfect methods.

### Cited Findings
- **Elbow test-retest SDD (electronic inclinometer, 2 raters, n=42):** about 4.2° (active extension) to about 10.8° (combined ROM). Active flexion was about 8.2–10.1° depending on upper-arm fixation — [Maastricht test-retest study](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/test-retest-reproducibility-of-elbow-goniometric-measurements-in-/); [PubMed 21779866](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21779866)
- Universal-goniometer elbow ROM has a maximal error of about 10°. Error can exceed 10° with improper positioning. A review reports a maximal SE of 11.5° for total elbow ROM — [search-result abstracts; PMC3183177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3183177); [JOSR 2019](https://josr-online.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s13018-019-1177-y)
- Across five clinical goniometric devices, inter-rater MDC was 5–11° (not elbow-specific) — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/b642780d-ca4d-4047-a950-054fef33be7d)
- **Elbow ROM MCID/MDC after open arthrolysis:** MDC 8.2° (80%), 10.5° (90%), 12.5° (95%). Distribution-based MCID 14.1°, anchor-based MCID 25.0°, substantial clinical benefit 43.4° — [Bone & Joint J 2021, 103-B(2)](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.103B2.BJJ-2020-0259.R3). Full text not retrieved; this is one surgical population.
- **RULA lower arm:** 60–100° elbow flexion scores +1, and 0–60° or >100° scores +2. The bands are 40–60° wide — [ErgoPlus RULA sheet](https://ergo-plus.com/wp-content/uploads/9-Assess-Risk-RULA.pdf); [IFA/DGUV](https://presse.dguv.de/medien/ifa/de/pub/rep/pdf/rep07/biar0207/rula.pdf)
- (caller-provided) IMU-suit elbow RMSE vs optical is 2–7°, with suit bias often >10° (Perception Neuron, Choo 2022; meta-analysis 10.6°, Li 2024). Markerless elbow RMSD is about 7° with about −5° bias even with 10 cameras (Theia3D, Lahkar 2022). Ergonomics accepts ±10° limits of agreement. An IMU-vs-Kinect TOST used ±10° (Ceccarelli 2026).

### Inferences
- **Argument from combined error.** The reference (2-phone video 3D) is itself a markerless estimate. It has fewer cameras than Theia3D (about 7° RMSD with 10 cameras), so its error is very likely larger. The suit adds its own 2–7° RMSE plus possible >10° offset. If the errors are independent, the expected RMS disagreement between the two methods is roughly sqrt(7² + 7²) ≈ 10° before biases. A margin of 15° therefore sits about 1.5× above the agreement expected from two good systems, and below the 25° anchor-based MCID. This is a defensible "practical equivalence" bound if stated that way.
- ±15° is wider than the ±10° used in comparable agreement studies (ergonomics; Ceccarelli 2026). The write-up should say openly that 15° was chosen because neither instrument is a gold standard and only two consumer cameras were used, not because 15° is the field norm.
- ±15° is close to the distribution-based elbow MCID (14.1°) and inside the 95% MDC envelope plus inter-rater goniometer error. This supports describing differences below 15° as "within the range clinicians cannot reliably distinguish or would not consider meaningful". That clinical anchor transfers only loosely to signing.
- RULA-style bands (40°+) show that 15° is well below the resolution at which ergonomic posture categories change. This supports equivalence for posture classification, not for fine animation fidelity.

### Gaps
- No published animation or retargeting quality threshold in degrees (for example, for FBX or Mixamo retargeting) was found.
- No elbow-specific MCID for healthy or non-clinical populations was found. The 14.1°/25° values come from one post-arthrolysis cohort.

## Q3. How should an equivalence margin be justified, and which statistic should it bound (max deviation vs RMS/TDI vs mean bias)?

### Takeaway
Margin-selection guidance (Lakens; FDA) asks for an a-priori, substantively justified bound on a stated parameter, in raw units, set by clinical or practical judgment. At ±15° the most defensible primary claim is equivalence of **mean bias**, with **TOST on an autocorrelation-corrected CI**. Add an agreement-type claim of **RMS difference or TDI/coverage probability ≤15°** (for example, "≥90% of frames within ±15°"). A **maximum-deviation (whole-curve)** claim is the strongest but is hard to support with one take, and it would likely fail at peaks and during occlusion. Report it descriptively, or with a simultaneous band, rather than as the headline.

### Cited Findings
- TOST tests whether an effect lies within upper and lower equivalence bounds derived from a smallest effect size of interest (SESOI). Bounds can be in raw units, not only standardized units — [Lakens, Scheel & Isager 2018, AMPPS, doi:10.1177/2515245918770963](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2788930055); [TOSTER vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/TOSTER/vignettes/IntroductionToTOSTER.html)
- When theory or practical considerations give no bound, a fallback is the smallest effect the study can detect with its available resources. That is a weaker, resource-based justification — [Lakens 2017, PMC5502906](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906)
- FDA non-inferiority guidance (final 2016): the margin M2 is "the largest clinically acceptable" difference and rests on clinical judgment. Industry objected that this is vague, and FDA kept the judgment-based wording — [CMAJ explainer](https://www.cmaj.ca/content/185/3/222); [Pink Sheet](https://pink.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/PS119534/Final-Guidance-On-NonInferiority-Studies-Lacks-IndustrySought-Changes); [BJCP, PMC5510081](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5510081)
- **Maximum-deviation equivalence for curves.** Dette & Kokot test H0: d∞ ≥ ε vs H1: d∞ < ε, where d∞ is the sup-norm distance between functional parameters (for example mean curves). They use bootstrap quantiles, and the test is less conservative than pointwise intersection-union tests. It assumes samples of curves, i.e. repeated functional observations — [Dette & Kokot, Biometrika 2021, 108(4):895–913](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/biomet/v108y2021i4p895-913..html); [arXiv 2004.12364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12364)
- **Agreement criteria.** Lin's TDI is the p0-th percentile of |D| (p0 usually ≥0.80). Coverage probability (CP) is P(|D| ≤ δ0), where differences within ±δ0 count as practically equivalent — [Choudhary & Nagaraja 2005 preprint](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~pankaj/choudhary_nagaraja_jspi_2005_preprint.pdf); [Escaramís et al., PMC2859350](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859350)
- Lin's original TDI test may be inconsistent and may not keep its nominal level. Exact and bootstrap-t alternatives exist — [Choudhary & Nagaraja 2005](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~pankaj/choudhary_nagaraja_jspi_2005_preprint.pdf)
- A comparison of CCC, LoA, TDI, CP and CIA found similar conclusions across methods. It recommends CP together with a graphical display of the raw data — [BMC Med Res Methodol 2020, PMC7291585](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7291585/)

### Inferences
- **The three forms of the claim differ in strength.** At a fixed 15°:
  - **Mean bias, |μ_D| < 15°:** the weakest claim and the easiest to pass. Opposite-signed errors can cancel, so a near-zero mean can hide large frame-level disagreement. It is defensible at 15° because reported suit biases (>10°) and markerless biases (about −5°) could plausibly add up to about 15°. That makes passing informative rather than trivial. Pair it with LoA.
  - **RMS / TDI / CP:** a medium-strength claim about individual agreement. "RMS ≤ 15°" or "TDI_0.90 ≤ 15°" (equivalently CP(±15°) ≥ 0.90) captures both bias and spread. Given the expected combined RMS of about 10°, this is plausible but not certain to pass. I think it is the most defensible *substantive* claim at 15° for an avatar-fidelity argument.
  - **Max deviation / whole curve, sup|D(t)| < 15°:** the strongest claim. Single-frame spikes from occlusion (hands crossing the body, oblique-camera self-occlusion), fast elbow extension, or time misalignment will likely exceed 15° somewhere. Dette & Kokot's test also assumes multiple curve realisations, while one take gives one curve. If attempted, segment the take into repeated signs or tokens, or build a simultaneous confidence band on the difference curve. Otherwise report max deviation and the percentage of time outside ±15° as descriptives.
- **Single-take pitfall: autocorrelation.** Frames are not independent samples. A TOST on the frame-wise mean with n = number of frames will badly understate the SE. Use the effective sample size (AR(1) correction), a moving-block bootstrap, or per-sign or per-segment means as the unit of analysis. This is a standard time-series argument and not from a cited source here. Temporal misalignment between FBX and video, and the angle-definition mismatch between the FBX joint-local rotation and the video 3D vector angle, should be resolved before testing. Both inflate all three statistics.
- **Suggested justification text** (synthesis): "The equivalence bound of ±15° was fixed a priori. It reflects (i) the expected combined error of two non-gold-standard instruments (published elbow RMSE of 2–7° for IMU suits and about 7° for multi-camera markerless systems, ≈10° combined); (ii) clinical measurement precision for elbow angle (goniometric MDC/SDD 4–12.5°); and (iii) the smallest clinically important elbow-ROM change reported (≈14° distribution-based). It is wider than the ±10° used in some IMU agreement studies, because our reference is a two-camera video reconstruction with its own error. The bound is not a perceptual threshold for signers."
- **Honest wording for "video as reference".** Call video the *reference method* or *comparator*, not "ground truth". Frame the analysis as method agreement (Bland–Altman, LoA, TDI/CP) between two imperfect instruments. Equivalence then means "the suit agrees with the video reconstruction within ±15°", not "the suit is accurate to 15°". If possible, report the video pipeline's own error, for example its agreement with published markerless benchmarks or a check on a static, goniometer-measured pose. A single performance by a single signer limits generalisation, and the write-up should say this.

### Gaps
- No published guidance found that states which of mean, RMS or sup-norm a kinematic equivalence margin should bound. The recommendation above is reasoned from the properties of each statistic.
- I could not retrieve the full text of Lakens et al. 2018, so the exact wording of its taxonomy of SESOI justifications (for example, "objective" vs "subjective" justification, measurement precision) is unconfirmed.
- No worked example found of Dette–Kokot or TDI applied to a single continuous kinematic trial with autocorrelation.
