# Software-only angle-definition alignment and honest re-analysis of already-seen takes

Scope: (a) how to align joint-angle definitions between the Rokoko/Mixamo FBX skeleton and RTMW video keypoints using only the recorded data (After, Action, and their FBX files), and (b) how to re-run the equivalence analysis on those same, already-analysed takes with new code without overstating the claim. Labels used below: **[cited]** means a sourced fact. **[in-house]** means reasoning or arithmetic by this researcher that is not taken from a source. Project facts in the assignment (nine unsigned angles, ±15° RMS margin, Monte Carlo, pre-sign-hold offset rule, fixed sequence) are taken as given and not re-cited.

Bottom line: no software change made now can turn After or Action into confirmatory evidence. Both takes, and the direction of their differences, have been seen. The honest options are (1) a re-analysis that is clearly labelled exploratory, made as disciplined as possible (blinded tuning, a pipeline frozen in a commit, a multiverse table), and (2) a pipeline frozen now that becomes confirmatory for the next take. That second step needs new data, which the user has ruled out, so it is only a recommendation for the future.

## 1. Virtual markers on the FBX skeleton that match video keypoint semantics

### Takeaway
There is strong evidence that video keypoints are not anatomical joint centres. Systematic shoulder and hip offsets of about 2.5–5 cm against marker-based joint centres have been measured. Mapping one point set onto the other's semantics, as OpenCap's marker enhancer does in the video-to-anatomy direction, reduces joint-angle error substantially. The reverse direction (building keypoint-like virtual points on an IMU or FBX skeleton) is technically simple and has precedent in OpenSim workflows. However, I found no study that measures its effect on suit-vs-video disagreement, so for SignSure it is a plausible but untested fix.

### Cited Findings
- Needham et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) compared OpenPose, AlphaPose and DeepLabCut 3D joint centres with marker-based centres. The mean systematic shoulder difference was 24–34 mm, and OpenPose gave 29/27/32 mm for walking/running/jumping. Hip differences were 29–53 mm, with every method placing the hip "more lateral and inferior" than the marker-based estimate. The authors attribute this to "mislabeling of ground truth data in the training datasets" (a hypothesis, not tested), and they did not test any correction. — [Needham et al. 2021, PMC8526586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526586/)
- The same paper says it is "unrealistic to expect a pose estimation model trained on open access data sets" to locate anatomically accurate joint centres. It also notes that marker-based joint centres are not a true gold standard (biplanar videoradiography is). Elbow and wrist offsets were not reported. — [Needham et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526586/)
- OpenCap (Uhlrich et al. 2023, *PLoS Comput Biol* 19:e1011462) uses an LSTM "marker enhancer" that predicts 43 anatomical markers from 20 video keypoints. It has separate arm and body networks; the arm network uses seven arm and shoulder keypoints. It was trained on about 108 h of marker-based motion capture processed in OpenSim 4.3. Compared with using keypoints directly, it reduced kinematic errors by 4.2° on average and by up to 35.9° for some degrees of freedom. It performed poorly on movements outside its training data. — [Falisse/Uhlrich et al., "Marker Data Enhancement for Markerless Motion Capture" (PMC12198419)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12198419/); [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.13.603382.full.pdf)
- The follow-up enhancer (linear, LSTM and transformer variants trained on a larger dataset; the best one is deployed in OpenCap) reported a mean error of 4.1° (max 6.7°) on unseen movements, against 40.4° for the original enhancer. — [Marker Data Enhancement, PMC12198419](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12198419/)
- An OpenSim workflow for IMU suit data exported as BVH (a skeleton format like FBX) places virtual markers at joint rotation centres and on each segment. It then scales a generic musculoskeletal model so that model-marker distances match virtual-marker distances, after which standard IK gives anatomically defined angles. — [Sensors 2023, 23(12):5423](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/12/5423/html)
- The OpenSim documentation compares Xsens's own biomechanical-model knee angle with an OpenSim IK knee angle driven by IMU orientations, both against optical ground truth. The same raw IMU data therefore gives different angles depending on the skeleton or model definition. — [OpenSim Confluence: IMUs for knee flexion](https://opensimconfluence.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OpenSim/pages/53084209/Using+Inertial+Measurement+Units+to+Calculate+Knee+Flexion+Angle)
- Aligning coordinate systems between an Xsens sensor and an optical cluster: of eight methods compared, angular-velocity-based (functional) methods worked best, with an average error of 0.17 ± 0.08°. — [Mecheri, Robert-Lachaîne et al. 2016, IRSST](https://pharesst.irsst.qc.ca/etudes-primaires/73)
- Biomechanics treats the glenohumeral rotation centre and the most lateral acromion point as distinct landmarks. One fluoroscopy study trained an nnU-Net to locate both separately. I found no source on how COCO-style "shoulder" keypoints are defined anatomically. — [PMC10791975](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10791975)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] A constant point offset produces a posture-dependent angle bias.** The relevant angles are built from segment vectors, so displacing the shoulder point by a fixed amount d in a body-fixed direction changes the trunk-to-upper-arm vector by an amount that depends on arm orientation. With d ≈ 30 mm (Needham's shoulder figure) and an upper arm of about 300 mm, the worst-case direction change is about atan(30/300) ≈ 5.7°. If the video shoulder point also rides upward with scapular elevation while the arm is raised, but the suit's humeral-head point (Mixamo `LeftArm`/`RightArm` origin) does not, the bias grows with elevation. This matches the observed 13–24° shortfall in video shoulder elevation "only while the arm is raised". Mismatched point semantics are therefore a cause-level explanation, and an offset-plus-gain model is a symptom-level patch.
- **[in-house] Candidate virtual markers on the Mixamo FBX**, each defined from anatomy or the skeleton alone and fixed before unblinding:
  - *Shoulder (acromion-like):* a point on the segment from the clavicle bone (`LeftShoulder` origin) to the humeral head (`LeftArm` origin) at a fixed fraction, or the humeral head displaced along the clavicle direction by a fixed length. Alternatively, a point attached to the clavicle bone so that it moves with Rokoko's shoulder-girdle motion rather than with the humerus. The fraction or length must be fixed a priori; a value fitted to make suit and video agree would be circular.
  - *Hip:* displace the `LeftUpLeg`/`RightUpLeg` origins laterally and inferiorly, toward where video keypoints sit (Needham's direction). This matters only if trunk twist uses the hip line.
  - *Hand:* already done (knuckle centre on both sides). If the finger bones are static because no gloves were worn, the knuckle points are rigid offsets of the `Hand` bone. That is still semantically consistent but adds no finger information.
- **[in-house] A less circular way to set a virtual-marker parameter:** choose it to match a geometric quantity in the still hold, not the tested angle. Examples are bi-shoulder width (suit vs. video triangulated) or shoulder-to-elbow length. This still uses suit and video together, but it compares a length in a static pose, not the angle trajectory under test. Declare it as a calibration step and re-estimate it inside every Monte Carlo draw, as the offset rule already is.
- **[in-house]** An OpenCap-style learned enhancer on the video side is not usable as-is. It needs paired training data, the original model generalised poorly to unseen movements, and signing is far from gait or rehab movements. It is evidence that semantics matter, not a ready tool for SignSure.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that builds keypoint-semantic virtual markers on an IMU-suit skeleton and quantifies the resulting drop in suit-vs-video angle disagreement.
- I found no study of systematic elbow or wrist keypoint offsets (Needham reports only shoulder, hip, knee and ankle), and no published definition of the COCO or RTMW shoulder keypoint relative to the acromion or glenohumeral centre.
- I could not retrieve the Robert-Lachaine 2017 Xsens-vs-optical upper-limb paper's per-joint differences; the systematic-review table extract was garbled.
- I could not confirm how Rokoko derives bone lengths (actor profile vs. measured); this affects how anatomically meaningful any FBX virtual marker can be.

## 2. Posture-dependent bias models (offset + gain, errors-in-variables) and non-circular estimation

### Takeaway
Deming (errors-in-variables) regression is the standard method-comparison tool for separating constant bias (intercept) from proportional bias (slope), but its confidence intervals are known to be too narrow and it is sensitive to a misspecified error-variance ratio. The core statistical problem for SignSure is different: if offset and gain are fitted on the same frames that are then tested, part of the true disagreement is absorbed into the "correction". The estimand then changes from "agreement" to "agreement after the best linear recalibration". Non-circular estimation needs frames or takes that are not tested, and the two takes allow at most a two-fold cross-fit.

### Cited Findings
- In Deming regression, a slope confidence interval that excludes 1 indicates proportional bias, and an intercept interval that excludes 0 indicates constant bias. Ordinary least squares shrinks the slope (it ignores error in x) and so understates proportional differences. — [MedCalc: Deming regression](https://www.medcalc.org/en/book/deming-regression.php); [valytics vignette](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/valytics/vignettes/deming-regression.html)
- In Linnet's (1993) simulations, Deming slope and intercept estimates showed little bias, but their standard errors were underestimated, giving intervals that are too narrow and tests that are too liberal. Misspecifying the error-variance ratio λ biases the slope (Linnet 1998). Jackknife CIs are the usual remedy. — [valytics vignette (secondary summary of Linnet)](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/valytics/vignettes/deming-regression.html); [MedCalc](https://www.medcalc.org/en/book/deming-regression.php)
- A 2025 preprint reports that Linnet's weighted Deming works well when the true line is close to identity and correlation is high, but "may be deficient otherwise". It is a single preprint. — [arXiv 2508.02888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02888)
- Passing–Bablok, not Deming, is said to be the default method-comparison regression in laboratory-medicine guidance. This is a secondary source; treat with caution. — [analyse-it: choosing a regression](https://analyse-it.com/learn/choosing-a-regression-for-method-comparison)
- Data splitting (attributed to Cox 1975) protects inference after selection by partitioning units into a selection set and an inference set, at the cost of effective sample size and power. — [Selective inference in growth curve models, arXiv 2609.19573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19573)
- Data fission (Leiner, Duan, Wasserman, Ramdas; JASA 2025) splits a single observation into two pieces by adding external noise. The pieces are independent (P1 fission) only in special families such as Gaussian location and Poisson. Inference must use the inference piece's distribution conditional on the selection piece. — [arXiv 2112.11079](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2112.11079); [discussion, arXiv 2409.03069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03069)
- Comparable IMU-vs-optical work reports large between-subject angle deviations (0–22° in knee flexion) and a trend toward higher IMU angles, which shows that IMU-vs-reference bias is not one universal constant. — [Current Issues in Sport Science 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/e93da7bf286b4dd19bb0eb086a5d0eb9)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Circularity in numbers.** If gain b and offset a are fitted by least squares or Deming on the tested sign frames, the residual RMS is, by construction, the smallest RMS any linear recalibration can reach. A suit that over-reads elevation by 30% would then pass at the ±15° RMS margin even though its raw readings disagree. This is the method-comparison distinction between *bias* and *precision*: fitting on tested data leaves only a precision claim. If used at all, it must be reported as a separate, explicitly named estimand ("agreement after linear recalibration"), never as the primary RMS claim.
- **[in-house] Non-circular estimation options with the existing data, from most to least defensible:**
  1. **Physically defined correction (no fitting):** the virtual markers from section 1, fixed from anatomy. This involves no parameter estimation on the tested quantity.
  2. **Cross-take cross-fitting:** estimate offset and gain on Action, apply them unchanged to After, and the reverse. Valid only if the definition difference is the same across takes: same performer, same suit calibration, similar postures. The Action and After takes cover different arm ranges, and a gain extrapolated outside the range it was fitted on is unreliable. Two folds give no estimate of the cross-take variability of the correction itself.
  3. **Hold-based two-point calibration:** a gain can be identified from holds only if there are at least two holds at clearly different postures. After has pre- and post-sign holds, but both are probably near rest, so the gain is unidentified or very noisy. Action has only a post-sign hold, so offset only. Use holds for offsets; do not fit a gain from two near-identical postures.
  4. **Within-take temporal split** (fit on the first half of the sign, test on the second): frames are strongly autocorrelated (effective n ≈ 2–7 per take, a project finding), and the two halves cover different postures. This is weak, and with a single sign it amounts to extrapolation.
  5. **Bias as a function of another angle** (e.g. elevation bias against elevation): this is the same circularity as 1–4 with a more flexible model and greater absorption risk. Use it only descriptively, for example a Bland–Altman-style plot of difference against the mean angle to show the posture dependence, not as a correction before testing.
- **[in-house] Monte Carlo requirement:** any fitted correction (offset, gain, virtual-marker parameter) must be re-estimated in every Monte Carlo draw from the perturbed data, as the pre-sign-hold offset already is. Otherwise the uncertainty of the correction is ignored and the upper RMS bound is too optimistic. Deming's known CI under-coverage reinforces this.
- **[in-house] Simulation check for absorption:** extend `single_take_simulation.py` with a truth in which the suit has a real gain error (e.g. 1.3×) and the true RMS sits at or just above 15°. Then check that the offset+gain variant does not declare equivalence more than 5% of the time for the primary (raw-agreement) estimand. If it does, the variant can only support the separate "after recalibration" estimand.
- **[in-house]** Data fission is of little use here. The suit–video disagreement is systematic, not additive Gaussian noise that can be split, and the keypoint noise model is already used in the Monte Carlo.

### Gaps
- I found no published method-comparison study in biomechanics that cross-fits offset+gain across takes and validates the type I error of the resulting equivalence test.
- I did not retrieve Linnet's original papers; the CI-under-coverage claims rest on secondary summaries.

## 3. Re-analysis validity with already-seen data (blinding, pre-registration, multiverse, holdout, simulation)

### Takeaway
Blind analysis, as used in particle physics and advocated for other sciences, hides the answer while analysis choices are made, and anything changed after unblinding must be labelled "post-blind" and treated as less credible. For SignSure the answer (per-angle offsets, which angles failed) has already been seen. Blinding can therefore only partly protect a re-analysis. Credibility comes from (i) choosing settings with criteria that do not involve the suit–video difference, (ii) freezing the pipeline in a commit before recomputing results, (iii) reporting every reasonable variant in a multiverse or specification-curve table, and (iv) showing by simulation that each new step keeps type I error ≤ 5%. The result stays exploratory.

### Cited Findings
- Klein & Roodman, "Blind Analysis in Nuclear and Particle Physics", *Annu. Rev. Nucl. Part. Sci.* 55:141–163 (2005), is the standard review. A blind analysis is "a measurement which is performed without looking at the answer". In high-energy physics it was motivated by positive results later traced to faulty analysis methods. — [Roodman 2003 PHYSTAT, arXiv physics/0312102](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0312102); [citation via Golub, arXiv 2311.13542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13542)
- Costs of blinding: it "can severely inhibit the search for systematic errors" and can preclude studying unexpected events in blinded regions. A later commentary (Golub) is more sceptical of blinding's net benefit. — [Golub, arXiv 2311.13542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13542); [Roodman 2003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/physics/0312102)
- MacCoun & Perlmutter, "Blind analysis: Hide results to seek the truth", *Nature* 526:187–189 (2015): the data are modified so that analysts do not know the results until the analysis is mostly complete. Analyses done after unblinding must be reported as "post-blind" and are less credible. The authors explicitly do not call for mandates. — [Nature PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/526187a.pdf); [Berkeley News summary](https://news.berkeley.edu/2015/10/08/blind-analysis-could-reduce-bias-in-social-science-research/)
- Preregistration guidelines were built for new data collection. With secondary or already-known data, "researchers' hypotheses and analyses may be biased by their prior knowledge of the data". Templates for preregistering secondary analyses ask authors to declare what they already know. — [van den Akker et al., "Preregistration of secondary data analysis: A template and tutorial", *Meta-Psychology*](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625); [preregr template form](https://cran.r-universe.dev/preregr/doc/form_prereg2D_v1.html)
- Prior knowledge of the data is named as a key challenge for researcher bias in secondary analysis, and solutions are proposed for it. — [Baldwin et al., "Protecting against researcher bias in secondary data analysis", PMC8791887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791887/)
- Secondary data offer "ample opportunity for researchers to p-hack" (Weston, Ritchie, Rohrer & Przybylski 2019, as quoted by a citing paper; the original was not retrieved). — [Meta-Psychology template paper](https://conferences.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2625)
- Multiverse analysis (Steegen, Tuerlinckx, Gelman & Vanpaemel 2016, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*) reports the result across all reasonable data-processing choices instead of one chosen path. — [Steegen et al. 2016 PDF](https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/steegen-et-al-2016-increasing-transparency.pdf); [FORRT glossary](https://forrt.org/glossary/english/multiverse_analysis/)
- Specification curve analysis (Simonsohn, Simmons & Nelson 2020, *Nature Human Behaviour*) estimates all reasonable specifications, plots them, and draws joint inference across them. — [Nature Human Behaviour s41562-020-0912-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41562-020-0912-z); [FORRT glossary](https://forrt.org/glossary/english/specification_curve_analysis/)
- Sample splitting (Cox 1975) gives valid post-selection inference by tuning on one part and testing on another, at the cost of power. — [arXiv 2609.19573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19573)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] What blinding can still achieve here.** The analyst knows the old per-angle offsets and pass/fail pattern, so full blindness is impossible. Partial blinding still limits *new* forking paths:
  - The code computes and prints only video-internal and FBX-internal diagnostics (section 5) while variants are developed.
  - The suit–video differences are either withheld or shown only with a secret per-angle additive offset and multiplicative scale (a "blinding key" stored outside the analyst's view, a standard physics technique per Klein & Roodman/Roodman). This prevents the analyst from steering toward a pass.
  - Unblinding happens once, after the pipeline is committed (record the hash in the report).
- **[in-house] Recommended re-analysis protocol (exploratory, for After and Action):**
  1. Write a "prior knowledge" statement, following the secondary-data preregistration templates. It should list what was seen: offsets of 5–24°, the elevation shortfall when the arm is raised, which angles failed, and that Action has no pre-sign hold.
  2. List each candidate change (virtual shoulder marker, hip marker, offset rule variants, gain variant, multiple-testing procedure). For each one, give a justification that does not use the suit–video difference: anatomy, a literature offset such as Needham's, or a video-internal metric.
  3. Fix one *primary exploratory specification* and commit it, including `equivalence_margins.json` changes, before running it on the takes.
  4. Run the simulation suite with the new steps inside the Monte Carlo (section 2). Report the false-equivalence rate at the margin boundary for each new step, and drop any step that exceeds 5%.
  5. Unblind once. Report the primary exploratory result together with a full multiverse table: all combinations of {no / virtual shoulder marker} × {no offset / pre-hold offset / post-hold offset} × {no gain / cross-fitted gain} × {fixed sequence / fallback / Holm}. Show the fraction of specifications that pass per angle, and do not highlight the best one.
  6. Label every resulting claim "exploratory: data previously analysed". The frozen pipeline becomes confirmatory only for takes recorded after the commit, which is outside the current hard constraint.
- **[in-house] Holdout between takes.** "Tune on one sign, confirm on the other" is not valid here, because both signs have been seen. A weaker version is still useful: develop any fitted correction using only Action, which is already exploratory because it has no pre-sign hold, then apply it frozen to After. Report it as cross-validated but not confirmatory. The reverse direction can be shown as a robustness check.
- **[in-house] Specification-curve inference.** Simonsohn's joint permutation inference tests whether the set of specifications shows an effect under a null of no effect. It does not translate directly to an equivalence null (|RMS| ≥ 15°). Report the spec curve descriptively, with each specification's own Monte Carlo upper RMS bound, rather than adapting the permutation test.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full text of Klein & Roodman (2005), Steegen et al. (2016) or Simonsohn et al. (2020); the descriptions above rest on abstracts, glossary entries and citing works.
- I found no published example of blind analysis or multiverse reporting applied to a mocap or markerless method-comparison or equivalence study.

## 4. Multiple-testing alternatives (Bretz graphical, fallback, Holm): when switching is legitimate

### Takeaway
The graphical approach of Bretz et al. (2009) contains fixed-sequence, fallback, Holm and gatekeeping procedures as special cases, and all of them control familywise error *when the graph is fixed before the data are seen*. Switching from the pre-registered fixed sequence to fallback or Holm after seeing that one group failed is a data-dependent choice of procedure. It cannot be confirmatory on After or Action. It is legitimate only as a pre-specified rule for future takes, or as a clearly labelled exploratory column next to the pre-registered result.

### Cited Findings
- Bretz, Maurer, Brannath & Posch (2009, *Statistics in Medicine*): a graphical approach to sequentially rejective, weighted Bonferroni-type tests. Each node is an elementary hypothesis with an initial weight, and edges say how its α is passed on after a rejection. It covers gatekeeping, fixed-sequence and fallback procedures. — [Synapse summary of Bretz et al. 2009](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2032208962); [arXiv 1908.10262 (application)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10262)
- The framework generalises Bonferroni, Holm step-down, fixed-sequence, fallback and serial or parallel gatekeeping. Equal weights with full transitions give Holm; a chain gives a fixed sequence. These are secondary tool pages, so check them against the paper. — [metricgate: graphical approach](https://metricgate.com/docs/graphical-approach-multiplicity)
- The set of hypotheses rejected by a graphical procedure does not depend on the order in which rejections are carried out (Bretz et al. 2009). — [arXiv 2402.13719](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13719)
- Truncated Holm-type gatekeeping has a parameter that interpolates between Bonferroni (0) and Holm (1) for redistributing unused α. — [arXiv 1812.00250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00250)
- A prior SignSure research note (`research_notes/Improving single take equivalence/statistical_design.md`, section 1) already covers graphical and fallback alternatives; this section only adds the re-analysis legitimacy question.

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Why switching after seeing results inflates error.** With a strict fixed sequence, a failed early group spends nothing on later angles. Choosing fallback or Holm *because* the early group failed is equivalent to picking, after the fact, the procedure that rejects more. Familywise error control assumes the weights and transitions are independent of the data.
- **[in-house] Legitimate uses:**
  - (a) A new graph (e.g. fallback: trunk α/… → shoulders → …, with unused α carried forward), committed now with `equivalence_margins.json` and applied to future takes. This is confirmatory for them.
  - (b) For After and Action, a reporting table with one column per procedure (pre-registered fixed sequence, fallback, Holm, unadjusted per-angle TOST). The pre-registered column is the headline, and the others are labelled exploratory sensitivity analyses.
  - (c) Do not change the headline overall verdict (EQUIVALENT / PARTIALLY EQUIVALENT) on these takes based on a post hoc procedure.
- **[in-house]** For equivalence, each elementary hypothesis is an angle-level TOST, an intersection–union test with no additional α split between its two one-sided tests. The graph only allocates α across angles.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the original Bretz 2009 paper; the special-case claims rest on secondary pages and citing articles.
- I found no literature on graphical procedures for multiple *equivalence* endpoints in method-comparison or biomechanics studies specifically.

## 5. Internal quality criteria for selecting pipeline variants without looking at the suit–video difference

### Takeaway
Several video-internal criteria can rank pipeline variants without touching the tested quantity: reprojection error, bone-length constancy, cross-view consistency, test-time-augmentation (TTA) spread, still-hold jitter and synchronisation residual. Reprojection error is the most established, and bone-length variation is a documented markerless error source. However, I found no study showing that these metrics correlate with true joint-angle accuracy against a reference, and one paper found reprojection-based metrics insensitive to algorithm changes. They are defensible as *blind* selection criteria, but their link to accuracy must be shown in SignSure's own simulation, where truth is known.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 clinical upper-limb markerless study uses mean reprojection error per trial as an internal quality check, with acceptable ranges set relative to laboratory baselines, and checks whether error depends on impairment level. — [arXiv 2607.23608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23608)
- A 2023 trajectory-reconstruction paper reports the fraction of reprojected points within a pixel threshold. This metric barely changed between reconstruction algorithms, so it can be insensitive to differences that matter for downstream outcomes. The same paper adds bone-length and smoothness constraints to improve trajectory quality. — [arXiv 2303.02413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02413)
- A 2025 *Journal of Biomechanics* study reports that limb-length variation is often larger in markerless than in marker-based data, so bone-length inconsistency is a real markerless error source. — [HAL hal-04940397](https://hal.umontpellier.fr/IFSTTAR/hal-04940397v1)
- Multi-camera triangulation theory: the best achievable point accuracy improves with the number of cameras, and consistent reconstruction algorithms reach the optimal rate. This is theory, not a human-body validation. — [arXiv 1804.10448](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.10448)
- Needham et al. found substantial random (not only systematic) keypoint error, e.g. an SD of 21 mm for DeepLabCut hip in running. Internal consistency metrics could detect such random error, but a systematic semantic offset is invisible to them. — [Needham et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526586/)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] What internal metrics can and cannot select.** Reprojection error, bone-length constancy, cross-view consistency and TTA spread measure *precision and geometric consistency of the video reconstruction*. They can legitimately choose between sync refinements, calibration multi-start settings, filter cut-offs, keypoint confidence thresholds and TTA sets. They *cannot* choose an angle-definition alignment (virtual markers, offsets, gains): a semantic offset is geometrically self-consistent, so it leaves these metrics unchanged. Definition choices must therefore come from anatomy or prior literature (section 1), never from internal metrics or from the suit–video difference.
- **[in-house] FBX-internal criteria:** the glitch count from `trim_glitches`, bone-length constancy in the FBX (it should be exactly constant, which is a sanity check), and the stillness of holds measured by FBX wrist speed. These can choose FBX preprocessing blindly.
- **[in-house] Proposed blind selection metrics, all available in the current pipeline:**
  - median and 95th-percentile reprojection error on moving frames (from `refine_lag` and the calibration)
  - bone-length coefficient of variation for upper arm, forearm and shoulder width across the sign window
  - per-angle TTA spread
  - still-hold angle jitter (the noise-model input)
  - cross-correlation peak sharpness for sync
  - calibration multi-start agreement (spread of yaw solutions)
  
  Pre-specify a single composite or a lexicographic rule, e.g. "minimise median reprojection error subject to bone-length CV < x%".
- **[in-house] Validating the metrics without new recordings:** in `single_take_simulation.py`, generate synthetic takes with known truth and vary noise, calibration error and sync error. Then check that the chosen internal metric ranks variants in the same order as the true angle RMS error. This turns an unsupported assumption into a demonstrated property of this pipeline. It does not cover semantic offsets, which the simulation does not model unless they are added deliberately.

### Gaps
- I found no published study that correlates per-trial reprojection error or bone-length consistency with joint-angle error against an optical or IMU reference for upper-limb markerless capture. Their value as accuracy proxies is plausible but unverified in the literature.
- I found no published study on using TTA spread as an accuracy proxy for whole-body pose estimators such as RTMW.
