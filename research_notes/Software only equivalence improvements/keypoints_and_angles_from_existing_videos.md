# Software-only keypoint and joint-angle upgrades for existing two-phone videos

Scope: what code alone can do with the pixels already recorded (no re-recording) to improve
2D/3D keypoints and the nine unsigned segment angles, especially wrist deviation and shoulder
elevation, for comparison against the Rokoko IMU suit. Items marked **[in-house]** are reasoning
by the researcher, not findings from a source.

Preliminary ranked list (detail and sources in the sections below; ranking is **[in-house]**):

| Rank | Upgrade | Target problem | Expected benefit | Main bias risk | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make the shoulder point mean the same thing on both sides (pre-registered GH-centre estimate on video, or acromion-like point on the FBX), or move elevation to a definition that does not use the shoulder keypoint as the humeral origin | Shoulder elevation reads 13–24° low when the arm is raised | Largest possible bias reduction for elevation; direction is clear, size must be checked | Circularity if the offset is tuned on After/Action; labelling conventions are not anatomical | Low (code only) |
| 2 | Two-stage hand refinement: RTMPose-m hand (hand5, 256×256) on full-resolution crops of the original frames, fed into the existing TTA + DLT | Wrist SD 10–21° | Real gain mainly in the 1080p view (whole-body model downsamples the hand there); small in the 480p view | Different label convention / training sets than RTMW hand points; model change after seeing results makes analysis exploratory | Low–moderate; CPU-feasible |
| 3 | Per-frame, per-view, per-keypoint noise model (TTA spread and/or calibrated confidence) used both as triangulation weights and in the Monte Carlo | Over-/under-stated uncertainty; bad frames dominate | Better calibrated CIs; fewer gross errors | Detector confidence is poorly calibrated; weighting can favour the systematically biased view | Low |
| 4 | Whole-take skeleton fit with constant bone lengths (incl. wrist→knuckle length) across both views (bundle-adjust style), not per-frame DLT | Depth/foreshortening noise in 2-view triangulation | Moderate noise reduction for depth-dominated errors; little for perpendicular jitter of a short segment | Wrong fixed length biases angles; over-regularisation | Moderate |
| 5 | Ensemble a second model family (dedicated hand model, possibly ViTPose/Sapiens) with inverse-variance weights | Model-specific errors | Modest; uncorrelated errors average down | Systematic label offsets between families get averaged in | Moderate–high (CPU) |
| 6 | Temporal: keep zero-lag Butterworth; optionally RTS smoother with per-frame noise; avoid SmoothNet / one-euro for the reported angles | Jitter | Small beyond what 6 Hz already does | Peak attenuation ("blunts RoM") | Low |
| 7 | SMPL-X / biomechanical-model multi-view fitting (EasyMocap-style or Cotton-style end-to-end) | Everything at once | Uncertain for the shoulder; plausibility/robustness gains documented | Model joint definitions and fitted scale drive bias; heavy compute | High |
| 8 | Super-resolution of 480p hand crops | 480p hand too small | Weak evidence; helps only very small subjects | Hallucinated finger/knuckle structure | Moderate |

## Two-stage hand refinement on full-resolution crops (RTMPose-hand, WiLoR, HaMeR, MediaPipe Hands)

### Takeaway
RTMW's hand keypoints are its weakest part (hand AP 66.3 vs body AP 76.1 for RTMW-l at 384×288), and the RTMW authors attribute this to hands occupying a tiny fraction of the person box. A dedicated top-down hand model on a crop of the *original* frame recovers the pixels that the whole-body model throws away when it shrinks the person box to 384×288 — which matters for the 1080p view but barely for the 480p view, where the whole-body input is already near native resolution **[in-house]**. RTMPose-m hand (rtmlib/ONNX, CPU-feasible) is the practical choice; WiLoR/HaMeR are ViT-L/H 3D mesh models with no published low-resolution 2D-keypoint evidence and heavy CPU cost.

### Cited Findings
- RTMW COCO-WholeBody AP (whole/body/foot/face/hand): RTMW-l 384×288 = 70.1 / 76.1 / 79.3 / 88.4 / **66.3**; RTMW-x 384×288 = 70.2 / 76.3 / 79.6 / 88.4 / 66.4; RTMW-l 256×192 hand = 59.8; DWPose-l 384×288 hand = 62.1; ZoomNAS 384×288 hand = 62.5; RTMPose-l 384×288 hand = 57.9 — [RTMW paper, arXiv 2407.08634](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634)
- RTMW authors: face, hands and feet "occupy a very small proportion of the human body and the image"; their FPN+HEM gains are "particularly for body parts like hands and feet with lower input resolutions"; ablation hand AP 56.4 → 59.9 (+3.5) at 256×192 — [RTMW paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634)
- Going from 256×192 to 384×288 input raised RTMW-l hand AP from 59.8 to 66.3 (+6.5 AP), the largest gain of any part — [RTMW paper table](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634) (interpretation that input pixels-per-hand is the lever: **[in-house]**, consistent with the prior project research)
- RTMPose-m hand (config `rtmpose-m_8xb256-210e_hand5-256x256`, trained on 5 hand datasets) reports PCK@0.2 = 0.815, AUC = 0.837, EPE = 4.51 on COCO-WholeBody-Hand val; it is top-down and needs a hand box (MMPose ships rtmdet-nano-hand, 320×320) — [MMPose RTMPose hand README](https://cdn05042023.gitlink.org.cn/OpenMMLab/mmpose/src/branch/main/configs/hand_2d_keypoint/rtmpose/README.md); [MMPose 2D hand demo](https://cdn05042023.gitlink.org.cn/OpenMMLab/mmpose/src/branch/main/demo/docs/en/2d_hand_demo.md)
- rtmlib can load this hand model as ONNX with `pose_input_size=(256, 256)`; Pose2Sim exposes it as a `Hand` (Hand_21) model via RTMLib — [rtmlib on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/rtmlib/0.0.14/); [Pose2Sim README](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- Community ExecuTorch export of the same hand5 checkpoint: fp32 ≈ 55.1 MB, Apache-2.0 — [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/mlboydaisuke/RTMPose-m-Hand-ExecuTorch)
- WiLoR (CVPR 2025): ViT-Large backbone initialised from ViTPose; 3D results FreiHAND PA-MPJPE 5.5 mm (HaMeR 6.0), HO3D-v2 7.5 mm (HaMeR 7.7); the paper reports no 2D keypoint PCK and no reconstruction-stage runtime; detector ~138–175 FPS on an RTX 4090; listed failures include small hands in crowded scenes and complex finger poses — [WiLoR arXiv 2409.12259v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12259v2); [WiLoR CVPR 2025 supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/supplemental/Potamias_WiLoR_End-to-end_3D_CVPR_2025_supplemental.pdf)
- A third-party paper measured WiLoR at ~21 FPS per sample and 138 GFLOPs per sample (GPU) — reported in search snippets of [Fast-HaMeR / related papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16444); not verified in full text.
- OpenPose and MediaPipe "frequently struggle" to detect hand keypoints in low-resolution sign-language footage (FG 2024; numbers not available in the abstract) — [Tasyurek et al., FG 2024](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/e0baeb7c-3cf0-44ef-aa53-5f181ad73caf/towards-better-communication-refining-hand-pose-estimation-in-low-resolution-sign-language-videos)
- In multi-view hand capture, two pipelines (triangulate-then-IK vs end-to-end model fit) gave joint angles differing by 12.1° ± 8.8° on average — reported in a search summary of [Biomechanics-aware multi-view hand capture, arXiv 2607.02796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02796); the abstract confirms the two-stage method failed to converge on ~15% of 121 recordings while end-to-end fitting solved all.

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Where the pixels go.** RTMW warps the YOLOX person box to 384×288. For a seated/standing signer occupying most of a 1080-pixel-tall frame, the box is ~900–1000 px tall → ~2.4–2.6× downsampling, so a hand that is ~45 px wide in the original 1080p frame is ~18 px wide inside RTMW. A 256×256 hand crop from the original frame sees all ~45 px (upsampled ×5–6). In the 480p view the person box (~400–450 px tall) is roughly 1:1 with the 384 input, so a hand crop adds no new pixels — only a model specialised for hands. Expect most benefit in the 1080p view (side view of `After`, front view of `Action`).
- **[in-house] Size of the effect on the wrist angle.** Direction error of a 2-point segment of length L px with independent isotropic keypoint noise σ px is ≈ √2·σ/L rad. At L = 20 px and σ = 3 px → ~12°; at σ = 11 px (the side-view TTA spread) the wrist segment is essentially undetermined in that view. Because the angle SD is linear in σ/L, halving σ in one view (plausible from 2.4× more pixels, not demonstrated) would roughly halve that view's contribution after triangulation. This must be measured with the existing TTA-spread machinery on the takes before adopting it.
- **[in-house] Keypoint semantics.** RTMPose hand5 and RTMW both output 21-point hands in the COCO-WholeBody-Hand layout, so the wrist and four MCPs mean nominally the same thing; but the wrist point in RTMW is shared with the body wrist (COCO body keypoint), while the hand model's wrist is the hand-dataset wrist. Use the hand model only for the MCP centroid and keep the body wrist (or test both) to avoid a definition shift in the forearm vector.
- **[in-house] Crop construction.** Build the hand box from RTMW's own 21 hand points + wrist/elbow (expanded ~1.5–2×) rather than running a separate hand detector; fall back to RTMW when the crop confidence is low. Apply the same 5 TTA (flip, scale, shift) to the crop so the existing noise model still applies.
- **[in-house] CPU feasibility.** RTMPose-m at 256×256 is a ~13M-parameter CSPNeXt model; on an M-series Mac CPU this is on the order of 10–30 ms per crop, i.e. 2 hands × 2 views × 5 TTA × a few hundred frames is minutes, not hours. WiLoR/HaMeR (ViT-L/ViT-H) are an order of magnitude heavier per crop and their outputs are MANO 3D meshes whose 2D projection is regularised by a hand prior — useful for robustness, not obviously for pixel accuracy at 20–45 px hands.
- **[in-house] MediaPipe Hands** is designed for close, large hands and returns no calibrated heatmap; not recommended for the 480p view.

### Gaps
- No source reports RTMPose-hand vs RTMW hand keypoint error on the *same* low-resolution crops; the gain must be measured in-house.
- No published 2D keypoint accuracy of WiLoR/HaMeR on small or low-resolution hands was found.
- Measured CPU latencies for RTMPose-m hand on Apple silicon were not found (estimate above is **[in-house]**).

## Super-resolution / upscaling of low-resolution hand crops

### Takeaway
Evidence that SR improves keypoint accuracy is limited and conditional: it helps only for very small persons/parts and degrades accuracy as the subject gets larger. A top-down keypoint model already upsamples the crop by affine warping; SR can only add learned texture priors, not information, so its main risk is hallucinated finger/knuckle structure that biases MCP positions. Low priority; test only as an exploratory arm on the 480p view.

### Cited Findings
- Hardy, Dasmahapatra & Kim (VISAPP 2022): keypoint detection improved after SR for people with a small initial segmentation area but declined as the area grew; the best result came from a Mask-RCNN variant that applies SR only below an area threshold — [arXiv 2107.02108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02108v2); [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2022/108637/)
- STAR-Pose (2025): pose-aware spatio-temporal SR for low-resolution video reports up to +5.2% mAP at 64×48 input and is 2.8–4.4× faster than cascaded SR + pose; the loss steers SR toward keypoint-useful features rather than visual quality — [arXiv 2506.16061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16061v1)
- FG 2024 sign-language work: U-Net/Attention-U-Net SR with a hand-keypoint-oriented loss "significantly" reduced undetected-hand errors on low-resolution TV news footage; the abstract gives no keypoint-accuracy numbers — [Tasyurek et al., FG 2024](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/e0baeb7c-3cf0-44ef-aa53-5f181ad73caf/towards-better-communication-refining-hand-pose-estimation-in-low-resolution-sign-language-videos)

### Inferences
- **[in-house]** Published gains are mostly about *detection* (finding the hand/person at all), not sub-pixel localisation. SignSure's problem is localisation spread (~11 px TTA spread), not missed detections, so expected benefit is small.
- **[in-house]** If tried, use SR as a separate augmentation in the TTA set (SR vs bicubic) and treat the SR-vs-bicubic difference as an additional systematic-error component, rather than replacing the input. A systematic shift of the MCP centroid between SR and bicubic is direct evidence of hallucination bias.
- **[in-house]** The 480p video is also re-encoded with variable frame rate; compression artefacts (blocking around the hand) are a bigger issue than resolution per se, and generic SR models trained on bicubic downsampling do not model them.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures SR effects on hand keypoint localisation error (as opposed to detection rate) or reports hallucination-induced bias.

## Multi-view-consistent estimation instead of independent 2D detection + DLT

### Takeaway
With only two cameras, classical robustness tools are weak: RANSAC/camera exclusion cannot vote out a bad view (removing one leaves an unconstrained monocular ray), so the realistic options are (a) weighting each view's 2D point by a per-frame noise estimate, (b) reprojection/epipolar gating that flags or down-weights frames, (c) a whole-take fit with constant bone lengths, and (d) model-based fitting. Learnable triangulation/heatmap fusion needs trained multi-view networks and calibrated rigs and is not practical for two uncalibrated, self-calibrated phones. Model fitting improves robustness/plausibility but moves bias into the model's joint definitions and scale.

### Cited Findings
- Pose2Sim triangulation: each 2D point weighted by detector likelihood if above 0.3; if reprojection error > 15 px, cameras are removed one at a time until acceptable; a point is skipped if fewer than `min_cameras_for_triangulation` (default 2) remain; gaps < 20 frames interpolated — [Pose2Sim README](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- A third-party study using Pose2Sim notes the 15 px default and that "the best triangulation is kept even if the error is above the threshold" for invalid joints — [Trampoline pose estimation, arXiv 2604.01322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01322)
- Pose2Sim accuracy (lower body, one participant): mean joint-angle errors 3.0° walking, 4.1° running, 4.0° cycling vs marker-based — [Pose2Sim Part 2](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03828641); robustness tests: sagittal walking errors 1.5°, 0.90°, 0.19° for image degradation, fewer cameras, calibration error — [Pose2Sim Part 1](https://hal.umontpellier.fr/LJK-GI-CVGI/hal-03581859v1)
- Learnable triangulation (Iskakov et al., ICCV 2019): algebraic triangulation with learned per-view confidence weights and a volumetric (3D feature aggregation) variant; both outperformed prior multi-view methods and a RANSAC baseline on Human3.6M; secondary listings give ~20.8 mm MPJPE (17.7 mm filtered) on Human3.6M — [ICCV 2019 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Iskakov_Learnable_Triangulation_of_Human_Pose_ICCV_2019_paper.html); [review summary](https://liner.com/review/learnable-triangulation-human-pose) (numbers from secondary listings, not verified in the paper table)
- End-to-end probabilistic fit of a MuJoCo biomechanical model directly to 2D keypoints across views (Cotton group, 2025): body scale and marker offsets estimated jointly with the trajectory; detector confidence sets the width of a learned error distribution (2nd-order polynomial of the score); 95th-percentile spatial error ≈ 20 mm; distal arm parameters "poorly tracked"; hands not used; 10–20 trials take ~1–2 h on an A100 — [arXiv 2502.06486](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06486v1)
- Follow-up (2026): bias-corrected median joint-angle error < 3.76° vs marker-based gait, with discrepancies attributed to model definitions (joint-centre locations, segment lengths); pelvis rotation intervals overconfident (~70% coverage for nominal 95%) — [arXiv 2601.22412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22412)
- Multi-view hand capture: end-to-end model-in-the-loop fitting solved all 121 recordings, while triangulate-then-constrained-IK failed on ~15%, with more plausible kinematics and better occlusion robustness for end-to-end — [arXiv 2607.02796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02796)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Two-view geometry.** For one point seen by two views there are 4 equations and 3 unknowns, so the only internal consistency check is the epipolar residual (one number). It can detect a gross error but cannot say *which* view is wrong. Practical rule: weight each view by its own per-frame TTA variance (inverse-variance weighted DLT or, better, minimise Mahalanobis reprojection error per point), and gate frames whose epipolar residual exceeds a χ² bound into "low-confidence" for the Monte Carlo, rather than silently excluding them.
- **[in-house] Optimal vs DLT.** Replacing DLT with weighted nonlinear least-squares (minimising reprojection error with per-view covariances) is cheap and removes DLT's algebraic-error bias when the two views have very different resolutions (1080p vs 480p): unweighted DLT effectively trusts the 480p pixel as much as the 1080p pixel.
- **[in-house] Constant bone lengths.** A whole-take fit (positions per frame + one length per segment shared across frames, solved jointly with reprojection residuals) removes the along-bone noise component and constrains depth where the two views are near-parallel for that segment. For the short wrist→knuckle segment the dominant 2D noise is perpendicular to the bone, so the length constraint helps less than for the upper arm/forearm. Risk: a wrong length (e.g., biased by systematically mislocated MCPs) biases angles; estimate lengths from the still holds, report raw vs constrained.
- **[in-house] Learnable triangulation / heatmap fusion** require training on calibrated multi-view data and access to heatmaps of a compatible backbone; with self-calibrated phones and no in-domain training data, expected benefit does not justify the effort.
- **[in-house] SMPL-X/biomechanical fitting** is attractive mainly for the shoulder (see next section) and for plausibility, but evidence above says bias then comes from the model's joint definitions and fitted scale; it is the highest-effort option.

### Gaps
- No direct comparison of SMPL-X multi-view fitting vs triangulation-with-fixed-bone-lengths on upper-limb joint angles was found.
- No source quantifies confidence-weighted vs unweighted DLT for the specific case of two cameras of unequal resolution.
- Epipolar transformer / cross-view heatmap fusion papers were not retrieved in this session.

## Shoulder point semantics: glenohumeral joint centre vs acromion-like keypoint

### Takeaway
There is good indirect evidence that markerless keypoints carry *systematic* location offsets relative to anatomical joint centres (30–50 mm at hip/knee from labelling conventions) and that shoulder angle errors from landmark offsets grow sharply near 90° elevation — consistent with SignSure's 13–24° under-reading when the arm is raised. Software fixes are either (a) estimate a GH-centre-like point from available keypoints (rules or a learned marker-enhancer), or (b) build matching points on the FBX so both sides measure the same thing. Both must be pre-registered and validated on data other than After/Action, otherwise they are fitted to the result.

### Cited Findings
- Pose-estimation-derived joint centres (OpenPose, AlphaPose, DeepLabCut) showed systematic differences of ~30–50 mm at hip and knee vs marker-based centres, "most likely due to mislabeling of ground truth data in the training datasets"; ankle 1–15 mm — [Needham et al., Sci Rep 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8526586/)
- Marker offset on the shoulder causes shoulder joint-angle errors that "should not be neglected" when elevation is near ±90° — [Shoulder joint angle errors caused by marker offset, 2015](https://opus.cloud1.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/117406/1/Shoulder%20joint%20angle%20errors%20caused%20by%20marker%20offset.pdf)
- Acromion-based GH-centre estimates shift with scapular motion (anterior in 90° flexion with internal rotation; posterior in abduction); spherical-fit functional methods most accurate for elevations near horizontal — [Functional Identification of Shoulder Joint Centers, U. Delaware thesis](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/068e980b-749c-47f6-857c-2b94f29fe373/full)
- A regression model for GH-joint location (MRI-validated) had 13 ± 2 mm error (Campbell et al. 2009) — reported via [UWA repository record](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/mri-development-and-validation-of-two-new-predictive-methods-of-g/) (requires scapular landmarks not available on video)
- Overhead abduction is accompanied by ~30° of anteversion, which affects how elevation is defined — [Rettig et al., BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2015](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12891-015-0840-7)
- Clinical markerless vs marker-based shoulder (100 patients, AAOS 2026 abstract): flexion peak-angle ICC 0.81–0.95, abduction-based models 0.35–0.97, flexion within ~10°, rotation underestimated — [AAOS 2026 abstract](https://index.mirasmart.com/AAOS2026/PDFfiles/AAOS2026-008260.pdf)
- OpenCap marker enhancer: an arm model maps 7 arm/shoulder keypoints (+ height, weight) to 8 arm anatomical markers; trained on synthetic pairs from virtual markers on scaled OpenSim models; arm marker test error LSTM 16.3 mm, transformer 15.3 mm, linear 31.3 mm; no upper-limb joint-angle errors reported; arm data ~152 h — [Marker Data Enhancement, PMC11275905](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11275905/)
- The original OpenCap enhancer generalised poorly to unseen movements (mean 40.4°, max 252°), the retrained one 4.1° mean (6.7° max); raw keypoints gave 9.6° mean / 43.1° max on benchmark movements vs 4.1° with the new enhancer — [bioRxiv 2024.07.13.603382](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.13.603382.full.pdf)
- Pose2Sim docs: the augmenter "tends to give a more stable, but less precise output", mostly helps with < 4 cameras, needs correct participant height, needs lower-body keypoints (COCO-style sets "won't work"), fails on NaNs — [Pose2Sim README](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- Model definitions (joint-centre locations, segment lengths) account for much of the residual bias between markerless model fits and marker-based references — [arXiv 2601.22412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22412)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Mechanism.** The COCO "shoulder" was hand-labelled on the visible shoulder contour; when the arm is raised, clavicle elevation and scapular upward rotation lift the contour while the humeral head moves less relative to the thorax. Elevation is computed from the shoulder keypoint → elbow vector, so a shoulder point that rises by d with upper-arm length ℓ at ~90° elevation lowers the measured elevation by ≈ atan(d/ℓ): d = 5 cm, ℓ = 28 cm → ~10°; d = 10 cm → ~20°. The observed 13–24° therefore requires a ~6–12 cm elevation-dependent superior shift (plus any trunk-axis effect), which is large but plausible when combined with shoulder shrugging in signing. This predicts that the error grows with elevation and is near zero at rest — testable on the existing takes without new data.
- **[in-house] Option A — redefine on the FBX side.** Construct a point on the Mixamo skeleton that behaves like the visible shoulder contour (e.g., a point on the `LeftShoulder`(clavicle)→`LeftArm` segment, or `LeftArm` offset superiorly in the clavicle frame) and compute elevation from it on *both* sides. Pros: no learning; both measurements then share semantics. Cons: (1) it only works if the suit actually measures clavicle motion — if the Rokoko clavicle bone is driven by a fixed or heuristic rule, the constructed point inherits a model, not a measurement (verify in the FBX: does `LeftShoulder` rotate during arm elevation?); (2) it changes the *claim* — agreement on "contour-to-elbow elevation", not humeral elevation; (3) the offset parameters must be chosen from anatomy, not tuned to minimise disagreement.
- **[in-house] Option B — estimate a GH-like point on the video side.** Shift each shoulder keypoint along the thorax frame (built from both shoulders and both hips) by a fixed fraction of shoulder width, possibly as a function of the measured upper-arm elevation. A learned version is the OpenCap arm enhancer, but it was trained on OpenSim-virtual keypoints (not real COCO detector outputs), is height/weight-conditioned, and has no reported upper-limb angle accuracy — treat it as exploratory. Any regression fitted on After/Action is circular.
- **[in-house] Option C — change the angle definition.** Measure elevation as the angle between the elbow−GH vector and the thorax axis, where the thorax axis uses the hips and the *midpoint of the shoulder keypoints at the pre-sign hold* (fixed in the thorax frame), so that shoulder shrug no longer moves the humeral origin. This removes the shrug component without learning, but discards true clavicular contribution.
- **[in-house] Pre-sign-hold offset rule does not fix this.** The existing per-angle offset is measured at rest; the shoulder bias is elevation-dependent, so a constant offset cannot remove it. A pre-registered elevation-dependent correction (Options A–C) is needed, and per the project's rules it is confirmatory only for takes analysed after the change is committed.
- **[in-house] SMPL-X fitting does not automatically fix semantics.** Standard SMPL-X fitting maps the 2D COCO shoulder to the model's shoulder joint (or to a nearby vertex); unless the COCO-shoulder→SMPL-X correspondence is a surface vertex near the acromion, the fit reproduces the same bias.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly measures COCO/RTMW shoulder-keypoint displacement relative to the GH centre as a function of arm elevation.
- No quantitative GH-to-acromion offset suitable for an anthropometric rule from video was found in this session.
- Whether Rokoko's clavicle (`Shoulder`) bone is sensor-driven was not checked.

## Ensembling model families and temporal methods

### Takeaway
Ensembling helps only if the families' errors are independent and their label conventions match; a dedicated hand model on crops is the most defensible "second family". Fusion weights should come from measured per-frame variance (TTA spread) rather than raw detector confidences, which are poorly calibrated. For temporal processing, the existing 6 Hz zero-lag Butterworth is a reasonable default; one-euro and acceleration-minimising filters are documented to blunt range of motion, and learned smoothers (SmoothNet) trained on generic datasets are a bias risk for fast signing movements.

### Cited Findings
- RTMW vs other whole-body models: RTMW-l hand AP 66.3 vs DWPose-l 62.1 and ZoomNAS 62.5 at 384×288 — [RTMW paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634) (supports the prior finding that RTMW-l is near the best whole-body hand AP; ensembling with weaker whole-body models has limited upside)
- Pose2Sim filter notes: Butterworth default 6 Hz order 4; one-euro "tends to blunt RoM"; acceleration-minimising (Whittaker-Henderson) "blunts the peaks"; Butterworth on speed "can introduce an offset"; Kalman, GCV spline, LOESS also offered; a Hampel outlier rejection step runs first — [Pose2Sim README](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- SmoothNet (ECCV 2022) is a temporal-only plug-and-play refiner; the authors argue low-pass filters ignore the "highly-unbalanced nature" of jitter and separate output error into jitter and bias; a comparison to filters is in the supplement (not retrieved) — [SmoothNet ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/1848_ECCV_2022_paper.php); [supplement](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136650615-supp.pdf)
- A 2025 report cites SmoothNet Human3.6M MPJPE 53.91 and MPJAE 0.98 — [Waseda 2025 paper](https://www.ams.giti.waseda.ac.jp/data/pdf-files/2025ITU_23B3_liu.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[in-house] Fusion weights.** Use per-frame, per-view, per-keypoint inverse variances from the existing 5-TTA spread (optionally per model), shrunk toward a per-keypoint pooled variance to avoid one lucky low-spread frame dominating. Report the between-model mean difference per keypoint on still frames as a systematic component; if it exceeds the TTA spread, the families disagree on *definition* and should not be averaged.
- **[in-house] Candidate families.** ViTPose/Sapiens whole-body models have heavier ViT backbones (CPU cost high; Sapiens uses its own 308-keypoint layout, adding a convention mismatch). The realistic ensemble is RTMW (body, wrist) + RTMPose hand (MCPs) — i.e., rank-2 above.
- **[in-house] Temporal bias.** Any filter applied before computing unsigned angles reduces jitter; but unsigned angles of noisy vectors are biased *upward* near 0° (folded-normal/Rayleigh effect: for 2D isotropic angular noise σ around a true 0°, E|θ| ≈ 1.25σ). For wrist deviation and near-straight elbows, smoothing positions before taking angles reduces this bias; smoothing the angle series afterwards does not. A Rauch–Tung–Striebel smoother with a constant-acceleration model and per-frame measurement variance is the principled version of the current Butterworth and lets low-confidence frames lean on neighbours; with constant noise it behaves like a zero-lag low-pass, so benefit is mostly on frames with variable quality.
- **[in-house] SmoothNet** is trained on Human3.6M/3DPW-type motion; signing has faster, smaller hand motions, so learned priors may attenuate genuine peaks. Not recommended for the confirmatory pipeline.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies joint-angle peak attenuation of SmoothNet vs Butterworth on fast upper-limb movements.
- No evidence was retrieved on ViTPose or Sapiens hand-keypoint accuracy at small hand sizes or on their CPU runtime.

## Time-varying confidence / noise models feeding the Monte Carlo

### Takeaway
Raw heatmap maxima / SimCC confidences are poorly calibrated as error estimates; calibration studies show a scale-dependent gap that can be partly corrected in closed form or with a small post-hoc network. The most defensible per-frame noise model for SignSure is empirical: per-frame TTA spread (already computed), optionally mapped through a confidence→sigma curve fitted on still frames, as the Cotton group does with a learned polynomial of the confidence score.

### Cited Findings
- Most 2D pose frameworks estimate confidence "in an ad-hoc manner" (e.g., heatmap maximum); the miscalibration appears as a scaling gap for heatmap methods and a form gap for RLE methods; closed-form fixes account for instance scale and visibility; a post-hoc CCNet improved AP by up to 1.4% and reduced downstream mesh 3D keypoint error by 1.0 mm — [Gu, Chen, Yao, ICML 2024, arXiv 2311.17105](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17105v1)
- In an end-to-end multi-view biomechanical fit, detector confidence sets the width of a learned per-keypoint error distribution (2nd-order polynomial in the score, softplus-positive); exponential likelihood performed best; calibration ECE (50th, 95th percentile) improved from (0.33, 0.43) to (5e-4, 0.095) as the minimum likelihood width rose from 0 to 2 px — [arXiv 2502.06486](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06486v1)
- Pose2Sim uses a hard likelihood threshold (0.3) and confidence weighting in triangulation, plus reprojection-error-based camera exclusion — [Pose2Sim README](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- The same group's 2026 follow-up found some intervals overconfident (pelvis ~70% coverage for nominal 95%) — [arXiv 2601.22412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22412)

### Inferences
- **[in-house]** Fit σ(confidence, keypoint, view) on the still holds (where truth is constant) and check calibration on held-out still frames; use per-frame σ in both the weighted triangulation and the parametric bootstrap. A floor on σ (like the 1–2 px width floor above) is important — without it the model was badly overconfident.
- **[in-house]** Per-frame σ will widen intervals during fast hand motion (motion blur at 30 fps, rolling shutter) where the current still-frame noise model probably under-states error; this makes the test more conservative but more honest.
- **[in-house]** Time-varying noise interacts with autocorrelation (effective n ≈ 2–7): per-frame σ should be simulated with realistic temporal correlation (e.g., AR(1) noise per keypoint, fitted from TTA residual autocorrelation), otherwise smoothing averages away noise that in reality persists across frames.
- **[in-house] Protocol note.** Any change to the keypoint model, triangulation weighting, angle definitions or noise model after After and Action were analysed makes re-analysis of those takes exploratory under the project's pre-registration rules; changes should be committed before analysing the next take.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that tests whether calibrated detector confidences improve two-view triangulation accuracy specifically.
- No source quantifies how much TTA spread under-estimates true keypoint error (TTA captures model sensitivity, not label-convention bias).
